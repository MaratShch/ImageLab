## 1. C38 — an adjudication where two of the three disputes were ours

C37 pinned three places where a vector re-read disagreed with an adopted raster set and raised them
as a decision. **Only one was about the film.**

### 5218 — the wrong document, and our own reader is why

`5218.pdf` is the four-page **brochure** H-1-5218. The adopted set is cited to the six-page
**technical data sheet** H-1-5218t (March 2006), which is a different file on the same disk.

⚠ **The reader could not open the technical sheet, so the sweep silently used the brochure.**
H-1-5218t p4 emits its entire page content **twice at identical coordinates**. `_sign_y_ticks` saw
`0.0 / 1.0 / 2.0 / 3.0 / 4.0` each appearing twice, concluded it was an unresolvable macron-minus
pair, and refused the page. C37 then found the panel on the brochure and recorded *"5218's page is 3,
not the 4 this row gives"* — **the page was right; the file was wrong.**

Dropping coincident duplicates (a genuine macron pair sits at two *different* rows, so the drop is
safe) and trying a second candidate frame, the technical sheet reads and agrees at
**0.033 / 0.082 / 0.056** — inside the band the eight agreeing sheets occupy.

**The adopted raster set stands.** The brochure is now registered separately as `5218_brochure` at
its own 0.241 / 0.210 / 0.138, labelled for what it is: Kodak redrew the panel narrower for
marketing, red peaking at 640 nm against the technical sheet's 650.

### 5231 — the wrong curve, and the reader picked it

The caption-to-curve rule assumed a criterion caption sits **above** its curve. True on H-1-5222,
false on H-1-5231, where both captions sit **under** theirs — so the `D=0.3` caption selected the
`D=1.0` curve.

There is no double-hump problem and never was. Paired by **sensitivity** instead — a lower density
criterion is reached with less exposure, so its curve lies above the other everywhere, which is
sensitometry and not layout — the trace peaks at **400 nm exactly where the adopted set does** and
agrees at **rms 0.063**. The curves are required not to cross before the pairing is used.

**The adopted raster set stands.**

### 5245 blue — the one real defect, settled against the plot

The stored tail ran −0.60 / −1.15 / −1.80 / −2.45 / −3.10: steps of 0.55, 0.65, 0.65, 0.65. A
straight line, which a dye tail is not.

Rendered at 6×, the yellow-forming curve plunges from its 460 nm peak and **leaves the bottom of the
frame (log S −2.005, the plot's own floor) at 527.5 nm**, where the stored tail still had it 0.8
decades higher. At 530 nm the stored tail claims a value *above* the last point the curve is drawn
at — which no reading of this plot can produce.

**Replaced by the trace.** ⚠ Red and green deliberately untouched: they agree at 0.064 and 0.155, and
they carry samples at 690 and 700 nm that the vector reader's 380–680 grid cannot supply, so
re-adopting them would have lost data to buy nothing.

## 2. K2 — `AimDensity`, and the two shape decisions are the data's

`AimDensity` (schema v21) is a **list** of records, one per published exposure index, each holding
four **ranges**. Both choices are forced by the sources:

- ⚠ **Ranges, not midpoints.** The sheets print "0.79 to 0.89" and never a single number, and the
  *width* is information: professional PORTRA quotes ±0.05 about its aim, consumer GOLD and ULTRA
  MAX ±0.10 to ±0.15, a pushed stock wider again.
- ⚠ **A list, not four fields.** PORTRA 800 publishes three columns (EI 800 / 1600 / 3200) and ULTRA
  MAX 800 two. Four scalars would lose the pushed aims or average them.

**13 stocks populated** from **16 tables across 13 sheets**, by a new registered audit,
`kodak_aim_density.py`.

⚠ **The tables are read by geometry, not page order, and that is not fussiness.** The PORTRA layout
puts both forehead readings in one merged cell and emits them light/dark *per column*; the ULTRA MAX
layout makes them separate rows and emits them *across* columns. A page-order reader transposes one
of the two, and both mistakes produce four plausible densities. Labels are corroboration only: two
sheets wrap a caption over two lines so no forehead label is findable at all, and every sheet repeats
"KODAK Gray Card" in a footnote inside the same band.

### ⚠ The E-190 (2003) PORTRA 800 table is broken, on two independent counts

1. **Its gray-card aim falls as the film is pushed** — 0.80–1.00 / 0.75–0.95 / 0.70–0.90 — where
   every other pushed table in the corpus rises, including the 400UC table four inches above it on
   the same page (0.80–1.00 → 1.00–1.20). Pushing develops further, and further development raises
   density at a given exposure.
2. **Its EI 800 forehead pair is copied verbatim** from the 160NC/400NC column of that same page:
   1.08–1.18 / 0.93–1.03. A 400-speed professional film and an 800-speed one do not share a forehead
   aim, and the two later editions give 0.95–1.25 / 0.75–1.10.

Same class as the E-2468 defect already pinned: a Kodak publication reusing a block it should have
replaced. **E-4040's numbers adopted; the 2003 table read, pinned and not stored** — and the audit
asserts it *keeps showing both defects*, an assertion inverted on purpose so that it fails if the
reader moves rather than the document.

Five of the audit's twenty-one checks are cross-document: PORTRA 400 identical across E-4050's 2010
and 2016 editions, ULTRA MAX 400 across E-7019 (2007) and E-7023 (2016), GOLD 200 across E-7022's
2007 two-column and 2022 one-column layouts, every pushed aim rising, and the 2003 defect standing.

## 3. K3 — the carrier already existed and was documented out of sight

The row said no record could hold a push. It was **right about `ProcessingFamily`** — a C-41 push
publishes no development time and `DevelopmentPoint` requires `minutes > 0` — and **wrong about
`ProcessVariant`**, which had held exactly the needed shape since v18: an alternate full response
with its own EI, curves and source. Its docstring said "a DIFFERENT CHEMISTRY", and nobody re-read
the fields.

So it was **widened**, not duplicated: scope becomes "the same emulsion under a different processing
condition", with a `push_stops` discriminator separating the two meanings. A fourth carrier with the
same five fields would have been worse. `push_stops` is redundant with `exposure_index` on purpose —
the stop count is what the sheet *prints*, and deriving it from the EI ratio would turn a printed
label into arithmetic; `FilmProfile.validate` checks the two agree.

**3 push sets on 2 stocks.**

### ⚠ The hard part was not the schema, it was which document

Three editions publish PORTRA 800's push and they disagree by more than the push moves some channels.
Red gamma at EI 1600:

| edition | red γ at EI 1600 |
|---|---|
| E-190 (2003) p14 | 0.6883 |
| E-190 (2006) p12 | **0.6100 — adopted** |
| E-4040 (2016) p4 | 0.6341 |

A push set read from one document against a base from another is partly an edition difference wearing
the label "Push 1". **E-190 (2006) p12 wins because its own EI 800 panel reproduces the profile's
stored curves to four decimals** (0.2200 / 0.5372, 0.6551 / 0.5185, 1.0072 / 0.6448), so the delta is
a push and nothing else. That anchor is now pinned in the audit alongside the two push sets.

### ⚠ And the newest sheet cannot anchor anything, for a reason worth recording

E-4040 (2016) p4's EI 800 panel has its exposure axis printed
**−4.0 / −2.0 / −3.0 / −1.0 / 0.0 / 1.0** across six evenly spaced ticks — two labels transposed in
Kodak's own artwork, confirmed against the page rendered at 6×, so this is the plate and not the text
layer. `_sign_ticks` refuses it because no signing of those labels is collinear, which is the correct
outcome: the alternative is a fitted axis wrong by a decade in the middle of the plot.

That refusal is now **asserted** (`EXPECTED_UNREADABLE`), because the day it starts reading is the day
either a corrected edition arrived or the tick fitter stopped refusing a bad axis, and those need
opposite responses.

### ⚠ 400UC carries its own EI 400 record, and that is the rule working

Its stored curves cite E-4035, which is not in the corpus; the push panel is on E-190 (2003) p13
beside that sheet's own EI 400 panel, which reads dmin 0.3338 / 0.7630 / 1.0508 against the stored
0.21 / 0.20 / 0.20. Storing only the push would make the difference between profile and variant a
difference between publications. Both panels from that page are stored, and the pair is internally
consistent.

⚠ Noted, not acted on: 0.21 / 0.20 / 0.20 is three near-equal dmins on a **masked** colour negative —
no mask at all — which is the signature B4 found on 5247_1983. Recorded in the variant note; nothing
here changes those curves.

Ten push panels are pinned in `kodak_still_curves.py`, **five of them precisely because they are not
adopted**: an unpinned disagreement is a claim nobody can re-check.
