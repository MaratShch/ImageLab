# Verification of the "classics / franchises film stock" list (2026-08-01)

> Status note 2026-08-02: unchanged by the Soviet reference-book pass; kept
> as the standing record for the Kodak/Fuji motion-picture stock claims.

Sources: American Cinematographer (theasc.com), Kodak Chronology of Film
(official), Kodak press/blog, BFI, ShotOnWhat (secondary), cinematographer
interviews. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / PLAUSIBLE-UNVERIFIED / WRONG.

## The four systematic errors in the list

1. **Code reuse confusion.** Kodak reused 5248 and 5293: 5248 = 25T (1952)
   AND EXR 100T (1990); 5293 = 250T (1982, lived one year) AND EXR 200T
   (1992). The list mixes generations freely.
2. **Anachronisms.** Vision2 5217 launched 2004 (after Azkaban was shot);
   Vision3 5219 launched 2007 and 5207 in 2009 -- Order of the Phoenix was
   SHOT in 2006, so its "Vision3" claim is impossible.
3. **5295/5296 conflation.** "EXR 500T 5295" does not exist: 5295 = 400T
   (1986, pre-EXR), EXR 500T = 5296 (1989, after Last Crusade wrapped).
   Slocombe's actual Last Crusade stocks: 5295 + 5247 (AC interview).
4. **The Rogue One film-out story is a myth** -- it belongs to Dune (2021),
   Greig Fraser's digital-to-film-and-back finish (ASC article). Rogue One
   was Alexa 65 with digital grain.

## Addendum: Technicolor bipack refinement + renderer bug fix (same day)

**Bipack asymmetry now modeled.** The three B&W camera records were
physically different and now render so: GREEN -- single film, direct
emulsion-first exposure, sharpest (MTF 48); BLUE -- front bipack film
exposed THROUGH ITS OWN BASE, pre-diffused, fine slow emulsion (MTF 38,
finest grain, highest bloom gain 0.26); RED -- rear bipack film exposed
through the whole front film + the red-orange filter dye, fastest pan
emulsion, softest and grainiest (MTF 32, rms 8.2, clump 12 um). Matched
gammas kept ON PURPOSE: the Technicolor lab developed each record to
matched contrast for dye transfer -- identical curves are process physics,
not simplification. Tier T3 (qualitative facts documented in restoration
literature; numeric splits are estimates around the previous uniform
values).

**Renderer bug found by this work:** GrainSpec.rms_r/g/b existed in the
schema and _grain_v2 filled them for every colour negative (blue layer
1.3x, red 1.1x -- top-layer physics), but film_sim passed the scalar
rms_granularity to all three channels, silently discarding the per-layer
values. Fixed: the renderer now uses rms_rgb(). Effect: every colour
negative's blue channel carries its intended extra grain; Technicolor's
three records diverge. Verified: full 67-check suite passes; 5219
flat-field channel sigmas now order b > r > g as the schema always
intended.
