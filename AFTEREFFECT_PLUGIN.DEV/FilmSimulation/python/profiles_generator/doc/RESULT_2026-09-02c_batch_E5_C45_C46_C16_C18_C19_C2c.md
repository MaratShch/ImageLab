# RESULT 2026-09-02c — E5 traced, six decisions taken, and three of the rows were wrong

Owner-approved batch: **E5, C45, C46, C16, C18, C19, C2c**, run without pause.

⚠ **THE HEADLINE IS NOT THE TRACE.** One row (E5) was ordinary work and produced a measurement.
**Three of the six decision rows turned out to rest on a mistake in the row itself** — a comparison
of a frequency with a length, an "invented constant" that cancels, and a recommended option whose
cost the row understated by a factor of three. Those are the results; the decisions follow from
them.

## 1. E5 — a good trace, withdrawn by a guard, and a convention nobody had written down

Source: `PDF/PROFILES/KODAK/Sehlin_Kennel_etal_1983_ChoosingECN5247or52941.pdf` — R. Sehlin,
G. Kennel et al., *"Choosing between EASTMAN Color Negative Films 5247 and 5294"*, **SMPTE Journal
94(7) 724–731, July 1985**. ⚠ The file name says 1983 and is wrong; the queue already recorded that.
Eleven pages, **no text layer**, one ≈285 ppi raster per page. Reader: `sehlin_kennel_1985.py`,
registered in the build audit.

**Fig. 8 puts DENSITY and RMS GRANULARITY on one shared log-exposure abscissa for 5294.** That is
the σ(D) construction complete on a single plate — no second document, no cross-sheet registration.
`NotFound.md` row 2 has asked for exactly this since 2026-08-17 and every candidate found since had
been a Kodak vendor sheet for a VISION-family stock.

⚠ **Two obstacles, both recorded in the reader rather than smoothed over:**

- **The two ordinates are not the same scale** — 218 px per density unit against 242 px per
  granularity decade — so each is calibrated on its own printed labels and never on the other's
  gridline.
- **The two curves cross near D = 1.0**, and no single-track follower survives that: within a few
  pixels of the crossing the nearest ink *is* the other curve, and a tolerance tight enough to
  refuse it also refuses the right one. They are walked **together**, the candidate pair assigned
  to the prediction pair by least cost, which works because they cross with opposite slopes.

735 of 799 columns survive the `|dD/dlogE| ≥ 0.25` conditioning gate (the rule the 7266 trace set).

**Traced, and validated — then ⚠ WITHDRAWN. The shape:**

| anchor | value |
|---|---|
| toe | **1.571** @ D 0.44 |
| mid | 1.000 |
| dmax | **0.703** @ D 2.08 |
| interior peak | **1.664** @ D 0.53 |
| stored? | ⚠ **no — see below** |

⚠ **It validates against a family it was not fitted to.** Peak 1.66 and dmax 0.70 land inside the
eleven vendor-sheet negatives' 1.20–1.62 and 0.50–0.90 — a journal plate, drawn by different people
for a different purpose, in the same envelope. Its maximum sits **earlier** (D 0.53) than all eleven
(0.65–0.80), which is precisely why `sigma_shape_peak` is stored per stock and not as a class
constant.

⚠ **AND THEN THE ADOPTION WAS WITHDRAWN, BY A GUARD, BEFORE IT SHIPPED.** Stored on the profile,
`cpp_parity.py` rejected it immediately: *"the RENDERED grain amplitude disagrees with the reference
by 5.66e-01 (tolerance 2e-05) at ('S', 'EASTMAN_5294_1983', 2, 0)"*. The cause is a **density-space
mismatch, and the corpus proves the convention itself**: on every measured stock
`sigma_shape_toe_at` sits at that stock's own **GREEN curve dmin** — 5219 0.59 against 0.58, 5201
0.62 against 0.62, 5245 0.57 against 0.64 — so `sigma_anchors` reads **per-layer analytical**
density. Fig. 8's abscissa is scene placement and its ordinate is the film's plotted density, and
the traced toe at D 0.44 falls **below 5294's green dmin of 0.68 and far below its blue dmin of
1.09**. The whole shape sat under the layer's own dmin.

⚠ **Withdrawn, not patched.** Re-anchoring needs a correspondence between Fig. 8's density axis and
this film's per-layer curves, and the paper prints none. **Shape and SPACE are as separate as shape
and level.** The trace, the validation and the refusal are all recorded on the profile, in the
reader and in a `verify.py` guard that asserts the absence — so nobody stores it again without
solving the anchor problem first. Measured count stays **13**; measured colour negatives stay **11**.

⚠ **The LEVEL is refused too.** The ordinate reads "RMS Granularity" with **no unit, no aperture and no
densitometry**, so it cannot be put on this corpus's 48 µm diffuse-RMS convention.
`rms_granularity` stays at 12.0. Shape and level are separate adoptions — the rule set on
2026-08-25b for `KODAK_TRI_X_REVERSAL_200`.

⚠ **Fig. 12 is refused with it.** Its MTF for 5247 crosses 50 % at **45–58 c/mm** against a stored
**estimate** of 24/28/33 — 1.6× to 2.1×. Not adopted, for two reasons about the document rather
than the disagreement: the running text calls it *"the **system** modulation transfer function"*,
which carries the printer and the lens, where the panel says *"5247 Film"* — not the same claim;
and **the curve does not overshoot**, sitting at 100 % out to 18 c/mm, where every colour-negative
MTF traced from a vendor sheet in this corpus rises above 100 % first because adjacency development
does that. Its f50 is a different quantity from the one `MTFSpec` stores. Same disposition as G6.

## 2. C45 — the clump scale: refused, with the cost of the alternative measured

Evidence in hand from TK1/TK4: five direct measurements, **0.87 / 1.77 / 2.46 / 3.22 / 4.64 µm,
median 2.46**, against 171 stored `clump_um_g` running 0.66–40.0, **median 13.0**.

⚠ **A second, independent route agrees.** Under the engine's own shape `h = exp(−(f/f_hi)²)` the
Wiener spectrum's half-power sits at `0.589·f_hi`, so a stored 13.0 µm implies **22.7 c/mm**. Ooue
Fig. 26 measures **45.6 / 70.8 / 140.7 lines/mm** on three named samples. The stored scale is
2–6× too coarse in the frequency domain as well as in the autocorrelation domain — the disagreement
is not an artefact of one conversion.

⚠ **AND THE RESCALE HAS A MEASURED COST.** Rendered grain rms (×1000) at a fixed requested 10.0,
across resolution:

| `clump_um` | 960 px | 2000 px | 4000 px | ratio 4000/960 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13.0 (stored median) | 13.9 | 17.8 | 18.2 | **1.31×** |
| 2.46 (measured median) | 15.4 | 24.2 | 29.3 | **1.90×** |
| 0.87 (finest measured) | 15.8 | 28.5 | 38.1 | **2.41×** |

At the physical scale a stock's visible grain amplitude becomes **nearly twice as
resolution-dependent**. That is arguably correct physics — real grain does become more visible as
you scan finer — but it is a change in how the product behaves, on 168 stocks, and it is not a
change the data alone can authorise.

**DECISION: not rescaled.** The row closes with the disagreement quantified from two independent
routes and the cost of the alternative measured. ⚠ **Three things would have to be settled together
before any rescale, and none of them is a document:** (1) `clump_um` and `clump_gain` are not
separately identifiable from an aperture series (TK1: a 2.6× swing in size for a residual that moves
0.013 G), so both must be decided at once; (2) none of the five samples is a stock in this database;
(3) the render-resolution behaviour above is a product decision. `verify.py` pins both medians so
neither can drift while the question is open.

## 3. C46 — Dufaycolor réseau: refused, because the argument for changing evaporates

C46's case for adopting Flueckiger 2018 Fig. 21 was that *"the new measurement needs no invented
4.05"*, the stored matrix having been *"multiplied by an INVENTED UNIFORM FACTOR OF 4.05"*.

⚠ **THAT ARGUMENT IS EMPTY, AND THE PROFILE'S OWN COMMENT SAYS WHY.** The derivation reads: *"the
whole matrix uniformly rescaled ×4.05 … **Rows are then normalised to a common row sum of 0.80**"*.
**A uniform scalar cancels exactly under a per-row normalisation to a fixed sum.** The 4.05 has no
effect whatever on the stored `filter_matrix`; it survives only in the prose.

What is left is a genuine but unadjudicable disagreement: diagonals agree to 0.015 / 0.056 / 0.009,
the blue element's red leak agrees to 2 %, and the red and green elements' leakage differs by up to
2×. Two prints of ninety-year-old material, of different age, one trace each.

**DECISION: stored matrix kept.** The Flueckiger bands stay recorded as
`_DUFAYCOLOR_RESEAU_FLUECKIGER_BANDS`, and the profile note is corrected so nobody re-litigates the
4.05.

## 4. C16 — coupler blur: all three listed options refused, and one of them is arithmetically wrong

C16 offered (a) render the edge term at higher internal resolution, (b) raise the shared threshold
to ~1.0 px, (c) replace the frequency-domain reference with the same truncated kernel. Its
recommendation was **(b)**.

⚠ **(b) IS REFUSED, AND THE ROW UNDERSTATES ITS COST BY ABOUT THREE TIMES.** The row says raising
the gate "removes a visible effect from every ordinary-resolution render". Do the arithmetic: the
edge term switches off when `edge_um·px_per_mm/1000 < gate`, so a 1.0 px gate at the stored
`edge_um` of 9–13 µm turns it off below **77–111 px/mm** — i.e. on **every 35 mm render narrower
than about 4000 px**. That is not "ordinary resolution", that is nearly all of them.

⚠ **(c) IS REFUSED BECAUSE IT MAKES THE REFERENCE WRONG.** For an already-sampled image, multiplying
the DFT by the analytic transfer `exp(−2π²σ²f²)` **is** the correct discretisation. The spatially
sampled kernel's effective transfer is the *periodised* Gaussian — the aliased sum over spectral
replicas — which at σ = 0.4 px is materially higher near Nyquist. The C++ kernel is not badly built
(it is `half = ceil(σ·ALGO_BLUR_SIGMA_CUTOFF)`, minimum 1, renormalised, so five taps at σ 0.4); the
divergence is inherent to sampling a sub-pixel Gaussian, not to the tap count. **Python is the
correct form and C++ is the approximation** — which is exactly the reference/production split this
project already declares.

**(a) is the only correct fix and it is not taken in this batch**, because supersampling the edge
term must be done *identically in both engines* or the divergence gets worse, and it needs a
single-thread performance measurement this batch cannot make.

**DECISION: C16 closes as answered rather than changed** — the question "which form is right" now
has an answer, the recommended option is withdrawn, and the residual is a characterised
reference/production difference bounded at 6e-5 above 1.2 px and 1.5e-1 at 0.4 px
(`interimage_parity` S09 ramp rows show 1.0e-2 to 2.6e-2 in the output).

## 5. C18 — the interimage weight is now bounded, and the bound is provably inert

`density_weighting` produces `w = (1 − dw) + dw·D_j/D_ref`, **unbounded in D**. It is the largest
undocumented number in the colour path: 0.65 on 36 reversal stocks, tier 3, worst-case correction
−0.58 logE on VELVIA 50.

**ADOPTED: `w` is capped at the value it reaches at that channel's own Dmax**, in
`film_sim.apply_interimage`, in `Algo_08_Sim.cpp`, and in `AVX2/Algo_08_Sim.cpp` (one
`_mm256_min_ps`).

⚠ **THE CAP IS PROVABLY NON-BINDING, WHICH IS WHY IT CAN LAND WITHOUT THE D1/D2 MEASUREMENT C18 WAS
WAITING FOR.** `ToneCurve.dmax` is not a stored guess — it is exactly the asymptote
`dmin + gamma·(shoulder_x − toe_x)` — and `density()` is `dmin + gamma` times a **difference of two
softplus ramps**, strictly increasing and bounded above by `(shoulder_x − toe_x)`. So `D < dmax` for
every finite log E, on both the negative and the reversal branch. **Every render is bit-for-bit what
it was.** `verify.py` asserts the proof's premise across the whole database (max asymptote − dmax =
0 over 171 stocks × 3 curves), so if a curve is ever given a dmax below its own asymptote the guard
fires before renders move silently.

What this does **not** do is supply the saturating *form* with a measured asymptote that C18 wants.
That still needs the wedge measurement; inventing a saturation constant would have been the other
kind of answer.

## 6. C2c + C19 — the rows compared a frequency with a length

`MTFSpec.adjacency_um` is the scale of a **difference of Gaussians** — `σ₁ = 0.4a`, `σ₂ = 2.0a` in
`FreqGrid.mtf`. Its band-pass peak has a closed form:

> σ₂²/σ₁² = 25, so 2π²f²(σ₂² − σ₁²) = ln 25, and
> **f_peak = 206.07 / adjacency_um c/mm** (a in µm, f in c/mm)

⚠ **C2c and C19 both compared a traced overshoot peak, in c/mm, against the stored value, in µm,
without that conversion**, and called the result "systematic, same direction". Once converted, the
claim does not survive:

| stock | stored `adjacency_um` | model peak | measured peak | inverted `adjacency_um` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KODAK_VISION2_50D_5201 | 16.0 | 11.4 | 10.7 | 17.35 (**+8 %**) |
| KODAK_VISION_200T_5274 | 18.0 | 10.8 | 16.1 | 10.65 (**−41 %**) |
| KONICA_IMPRESA_50 | 14.0 | 12.2 | 6.9 | 28.25 (**+102 %**) |
| EASTMAN_PLUS_X_5231 | 16.0 | — | 4.7 | ⚠ no interior peak at any length |
| FUJI_SUPER_F125_8532 | 13.0 | — | 9.0 | ⚠ no interior peak at any length |

**Scatter, not bias** — +8 %, −41 %, +102 %.

⚠ **AND THE CONVERSION EXPOSED A REAL DEFECT.** On **12 of the 161 stocks** that carry a nonzero
`adjacency`, the value is too small against that stock's own rolloff for the product to exceed 100 %
at **any** frequency: the parameter reads as active and is **inert**. Two of them —
`EASTMAN_PLUS_X_5231` (a 0.034) and `FUJI_SUPER_F125_8532` (a 0.090) — are stocks C2c cited as
evidence of a systematic disagreement. They cannot disagree about a peak they do not have. The
other ten are `GEVACHROME_605`, `EASTMAN_ORTHO_1930`, `ILFORD_HPS`, `SOVIET_PANCHROM_1939`,
`SVEMA_CNL_32`, `SVEMA_DS_5M`, `SVEMA_LN_8`, `SVEMA_LN_9`, `SVEMA_LN_9S`,
`FUJICOLOR_SUPER_F500_8572`.

**DECISION: nothing adopted, both rows closed on the corrected analysis.** The three invertible
stocks are not written, because the peak frequency and the peak amplitude come from **one** traced
curve and fitting only the length against a separately-stored amplitude is half a fit; the pair must
be fitted together, which needs the traced curves the sheet readers hold. What is stored instead is
the closed form and the inert census, both pinned by `verify.py` — which turns `adjacency_um` from
an uninterpretable literal into an invertible one, and that was C19's actual complaint.
