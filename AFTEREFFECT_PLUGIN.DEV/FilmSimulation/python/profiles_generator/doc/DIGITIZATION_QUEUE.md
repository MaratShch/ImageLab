⚠ **SAYANAGI 1959 SUPPLIES THE THEORY C44 WAS MISSING, AND CONFIRMS A CONVENTION C22 HAD TO ARGUE
FOR ITSELF.** «Callier Q Factor と粒状» (Canon, 23(1) 20-24) derives Q(D) from a Poisson model of
circular grains with **finite** transmittance T_g — finite because electron microscopy shows the
developed grain is filamentary. Three findings land on open rows: (1) his theory fits at **low**
density and fails at high, the exact complement of Silberstein & Tuttle which fits above D 0.3 and
fails below — **C44**; (2) T_g can be estimated from the measured Q at D → 0, which turns the toe
from a defect into a measurement; (3) ⚠ he states that Q must be defined on **base-subtracted**
density — «Q_II is the rational Q factor» — which is exactly what `callier_net` does on NET
density, a choice C22 recorded as having **no source behind it**. There is one, from 1959. He also
proves that Q contains the granularity rms but **not** the grain radius, an independent theoretical
confirmation of what C45 found empirically.

## 1. METHOD RULES — these bind every future entry

1. **Check for vector paths before rendering anything.** `page.get_drawings()`; a path
   with tens of `l`/`c` items is a curve, many images with only short paths means raster.
   Vector coordinates are exact and beat any trace by an order of magnitude.
2. **Two vector traps.** The Kodak logo is a filled ~30-item path cluster. Glyph outlines
   of headline text run 120–1350 items. Measured across the corpus: of 937 pages with a
   ≥30-item path, **421 were logo or glyph art** — always confirm against the plot title
   text near the path's rect.
3. **When the plot frame is filled rather than stroked, ignore the frame.** Fit the
   calibration to the printed tick LABELS via `get_text("words")`. Three Agfa sheets sat
   in this queue for weeks logged as "needs a hand frame" and never needed one.
4. **Classify tracks by persistence, not by stroke width.** Follow a candidate ±35 columns
   and count hits: **≥70/80 = solid curve, 25–50 = dashed curve, <20 = a text glyph or an
   axis tick.** Nothing shorter separates in-plot "B/G/R" labels from real data.
5. **Mutual exclusion between tracks, always.** One ink run may be claimed by one track per
   column. Without it two curves that converge merge silently — it produced identical Ḡ for
   two different development times on NEOPAN 1600, and a σ ratio of 44.3 on VISION3 5219.
6. **Bridge dash gaps only up to the MEASURED dash period,** never a guessed tolerance.
7. **Validate against a printed summary statistic wherever one exists.** Prefer plots that
   print Ḡ, CI or a speed. NEOPAN's three printed Ḡ values are what proved that trace.
8. **Measure a fitted statistic on the MODEL, not on the trace.** Average gradient starts
   where the curve crosses base+fog+0.10; taking that threshold from traced points instead
   of the fitted curve moved Ḡ by 0.04 and produced a wrong fit.
9. **Enforce `toe_k ≤ shoulder_k ≤ 2·toe_k` INSIDE the search.** A sharper shoulder than
   toe makes the difference-of-softplus model non-monotone past the shoulder — free fits
   with RMS as low as 0.0035 D produced real density reversals of −0.18 D/log H.
10. **Sanity-check siblings against physics before adopting — but check the physics
    against a SOURCE, not against intuition.** Three VISION3 sheets passed every internal
    check and still disagreed about whether grain rises or falls with density; internal
    consistency is not correctness, and that half of the rule stands.
    ⚠ **AMENDED 2026-08-17, and the amendment is the sharper lesson.** The physics premise
    used to reject that batch — "for a colour negative σ_D should RISE with density, since
    more developed silver means more grain" — **was itself wrong**. Colour negatives fall:
    the four sheets trace to dmax/mid 0.55–0.63 and Kodak's own SMPTE Journal paper of July
    1985 (Sehlin/Kennel, p 728, Figs 8–9) prints "overexposing either film significantly
    decreases granularity". An unsourced physical expectation is an assumption wearing a
    lab coat, and under rule 14 it loses to a vendor document. So: state the expectation,
    then find the source that confirms or kills it, before using it to accept OR to reject.
11. **Never resample a stored array onto a finer grid** as a substitute for re-tracing. It
    interpolates, adds nothing, and destroys the record of which samples came from the plot.
12. **Never synthesise a curve from peak wavelengths.** Three peaks are not a curve.
13. **After every adoption:** `verify.py` green, C++ regenerated and compiled,
    `film_names.txt` regenerated, docs updated. No exceptions.
    ⚠ **STRENGTHENED 2026-08-18 at the owner's instruction, because "docs updated" was the
    half that kept slipping.** The owner reads the markdown to see progress, and a document
    that was true last week reads exactly like one that is true now — the failure is silent.
    So the rule is now specific and partly enforced:
    - **`doc/PROGRESS.md` is the single status board** and is updated at the END OF EVERY
      TASK, before the task is reported done. Build state, what is done, what is open and
      ranked, and what is waiting on an owner decision.
    - It carries a `build-facts` stamp — schema version, stock count, `film_names.txt`
      digest — and **`build.py`'s docs stage FAILS if the stamp disagrees with the live
      database.** Fault-injected against all three fields and against deleting the stamp.
      An earlier version of that gate searched the prose for "v8" and passed on a document
      edited to say v7, because the string also occurred in an unrelated sentence: a gate
      satisfiable by coincidence is worse than none.
    - A dated `RESULT_*.md` still carries the reasoning; `PROGRESS.md` indexes it. Where a
      new finding supersedes an old status entry, the old entry STAYS and is marked
      superseded — the reasoning is the audit trail, not clutter.
14. **OFFICIAL SOURCE ALWAYS WINS over a third-party book** (owner directive, 2026-08-17).
    Ranking, highest first: **manufacturer datasheet / ТУ / ГОСТ** → manufacturer handbook
    (e.g. the Ilford Manual, published by Ilford Ltd) → independent third-party book. When a
    standard or vendor sheet contradicts a book, the book loses and the conflict is
    *recorded*, never averaged.
    **The corollary that bites, and the reason for the rule:** Soviet-era technical literature
    was written in scientific isolation, so its figures for *non-Soviet* films may derive from
    individually imported samples rather than from manufacturer data. Therefore:
    - Soviet books (Иофис, Чельцов, Журба, Гороховский) are **strong** for **Soviet** stocks —
      those authors had the ГОСТ and the factory catalogues.
    - The **same books are weak** for **foreign** stocks and must never displace a vendor
      figure. Where a foreign stock has *only* Soviet sources, say so in the provenance rather
      than presenting the number as documented.
    - ⚠ **The rule does not resolve a conflict between two Soviet books about a foreign film.**
      That case has no winner: leave the incumbent value and record both readings. See
      `AGFACOLOR_NEG_TYPE_3`, 2026-08-17 — Чельцов 1958 says ASA 20–25, Иофис 1964 says ASA 40,
      both Soviet books, both describing a West German film, and no Agfa source in the corpus.
    - **One empirical test exists, and it favours Иофис:** Double-X 5222 is the only foreign
      stock we hold with *both* an official Kodak figure and an Иофис figure. Иофис's table
      gives ASA 250/200 — identical to Kodak's own sheet. Consistent with his footnote
      «Таблица составлена по каталогам 1963—1964 гг.» One data point, not a licence.

15. **On a two-family plot, separate the families by DRAWING STYLE before tracing, and
    trace the family whose identity is certain first.** Kodak's granularity sheets put three
    density curves and three σ_D curves in one frame and distinguish them by how the line is
    drawn — dashed/dotted on 5207 and 5219, bold-versus-thin on 5203 and 5213 (5219 even
    prints a legend saying so). Splitting on style makes cross-family capture **impossible**
    instead of merely detectable; an ordering assertion cannot see a family swap.
    Connected-component width separates dash from solid, vertical run length separates bold
    from thin, and both thresholds sat in a wide empty gap between the two populations.
16. **Direction is a parameter, and on a tangential crossing only one value works.** Where a
    rising curve passes through another curve **at that curve's maximum**, both branches
    descend on the left side of the junction and no proximity or slope test can choose;
    approached from the other side one is rising and the other flat. Seed on the side where
    the curves are flat and known — for these plots, the dmin plateau at the left edge — and
    trace one way only. "Seed at mid-width and trace outward" is what failed three times.
17. **A FOLDER NAME IS NOT AN EMULSION IDENTITY, and a batch statistic inherits every film
    in the batch.** Added 2026-08-18 after `SVEMA_FOTO_65` lost three adopted values. A
    folder called `SVEMA-FN64` held 509 frames of which only 67 were that film; the analyzer
    reported one profile, the report said "509-frame batch", and for two weeks nothing in the
    chain could tell that from a single-emulsion measurement. Therefore, before adopting
    anything from a scan batch: **ask the owner which frames are certainly which stock, and
    write the frame RANGE into the provenance, not the folder name.** A mixture is worse than
    a small sample, because its bias is invisible and, where the films differ deliberately
    (here Foto-32 was chosen for finer grain), it is also **one-directional** rather than
    noise that averages out.
    **The corollary, and it is the cheap half of the rule: check the channels before trusting
    any per-channel number.** On the 67 confirmed frames `max |R−G| = max |B−G| = 0` —
    exactly greyscale. That one line, computable in seconds, voided a `base_tint`, a
    `silver_tone` sign reversal and twelve crossover bins that had all looked like precise
    measurements. For a **B&W silver negative** it is also predictable from first principles:
    developed silver is spectrally near-neutral, so its scan carries no channel information
    at all, and any R/G/B asymmetry is the scanner's illuminant and white balance. Run the
    check anyway, on colour material too — it costs nothing and it fails loudly.

18. **A CARRIER IS CHOSEN BY MEASUREMENT, NOT BY ELEGANCE — and the most accurate form on
    paper can be the wrong one.** Added 2026-08-18 from queue item C1. Scored against the seven
    measured σ(D) samples per VISION3 sheet, a 12-sample array reproduced them to 2 % rms while
    three anchors plus one interior peak reached 8.6 %. The array was still rejected: with seven
    measured points per film it is **fitting the samples, not the film**, and it would have
    presented five floats' worth of knowledge as twelve. State the sample count before choosing
    the parameter count.
    **The second half of the rule: a field nothing reads is not neutral.** `sigma_shape_*` was
    populated and validated for weeks while the renderer ignored it, and 137 of its 155 values
    came from a heuristic whose **both branches were wrong in sign**. Wiring it in wholesale
    would have replaced one wrong law with another on 137 stocks to fix 5. So a carrier that can
    hold both measured and estimated values needs a flag saying **which**, and the renderer must
    key on that flag — `sigma_shape_measured`. Populating a field is not the same as earning the
    right to render from it.

19. **A FRAGMENT THAT SURVIVES THE COUNT TEST IS WORSE THAN ONE THAT FAILS IT.** Added
    2026-08-18 from queue item C1c. The vector granularity extractor asked "are there six
    curve-shaped things inside the frame?" and on the 5279 sheet the answer was yes — five whole
    curves plus **one curve's right-hand third**, which spanned 67 % of the frame on its own and
    so passed every geometric filter. The sheet reported success and two thirds of a curve were
    discarded silently. Three sibling defects had the same shape: a group of ≤ 4 points dropped
    before any filter saw it (flat dashes are 2-point segments), a per-path item floor that
    discarded two of a curve's three pieces, and a `>= 0.0` x-step test that refused to join
    pieces which *abut* (measured step −0.1 pt). **Count tests must be paired with a coverage
    test** — this extractor now prints each curve's x coverage as a percentage of the frame —
    and stitching must always run, never be skipped because the count already looks right.
    **And the corollary that keeps being re-learned: the OVERLAY is the gate, not the numbers.**
    Every one of these six defects produced internally consistent, plausible numbers.

20. **AN ASSERTION THAT CAN ONLY MEAN "YOUR COPY IS INCOMPLETE" MUST NOT FAIL.** Added

22. **THE BEST PANEL ON A SHEET IS NOT ALWAYS THE ONE WITH THE RIGHT TITLE** (added 2026-08-20, from

    samples over 4.1 decades** and reach both ends.

      what only it states — here the abscissa origin ("'0' on the x-axis represents normal exposure
      of an 18-percent gray card"), which is **one** parameter. Six points are ample for one and
      hopeless for six.

      choosing the model, and count the free parameters before believing the residual.**

## 5. TOOLS

- **`digitize_plot.py`** — 600 dpi render, frame/tick detection, seeded ink-centroid
  tracing, least-squares ToneCurve fit. numpy + Pillow only.
- **`dye_density.py`** — spectral dye density from Kodak/Fuji vector paths. Three normalisation
  families: as-printed + neutral, peak-normalised triple, and (added 2026-08-25, queue C9)
  **peak-normalised + neutral + dmin**, identified by INK and validated by
  `Neutral − Dmin = k·(C+M+Y)` with the three k equal. ⚠ Its `n < 8` segment filter is deliberate
  and stays: the 7-segment overprint traces it drops are recovered by the ink reader instead, which
  knows what it is looking at. `--assert` reproduces 12 sheets.
- **`dashtrace.py`** — dash-aware mutual-exclusion tracer. `trace_tracks` +
  `check_ordering` remain, and remain **insufficient on their own** (see rules 10, 15, 16).
  Added 2026-08-17, and these are what the VISION3 adoption actually used:
  `family_split_by_style` (separate the two curve families by dash/bold before tracing),
  `trace_predictive` (slope-predictive, one direction only), `check_cross_family` (the
  assertion `check_ordering` structurally cannot make), `column_runs_weighted`.
  Its docstring also carries a **correction**: the old claim that a log-axis calibration
  error "cancels exactly" in the σ ratios is only true of the axis OFFSET. An error in
  px-per-decade does not cancel, so that figure must be measured per sheet.
- **`plot_inventory.py`** — inventories every plot page in the corpus and classifies it
  vector or raster, then maps files to stocks by catalogue code. Replaces two
  non-reproducible page counts with measured ones and validates its classifier against
  three independently-known pages. `--assert` fails if the corpus stops reproducing the
  recorded counts; `--csv` writes the worklist. Run by `build.py`'s audit stage.
- **`mees_granularity.py`** — re-derives the B&W silver-negative σ(D) shape from Mees
  Fig. 302 (four negative emulsions, printed p866). See `RESULT_2026-08-18b_bw_sigma_d.md`.
- **`spectral_vector.py`** — spectral SENSITIVITY from Kodak brochure vector paths, added
  2026-08-25 (queue C10). The first extractor for that panel type; every earlier spectral set
  came from a raster batch or from `agfa_vista.py`'s dash legend. Assigns layers by Kodak's INK
  convention — each trace is drawn in the colour of light it responds to, and the red record is a
  **yellow-under-magenta overprint** whose two paths must be bit-identical or the sheet is rejected.
  Reuses `dye_density`'s palette reader (`_ink`, `extract_inked`, `_fit_axis`) rather than
  re-deriving it. Cross-checks every assignment three ways that are not the ink: legend swatches,
  absorption bands, and the sibling sheets' independently-adopted sets. `--assert` pins the peak
  wavelengths AND the absolute peak sensitivities, which the schema's per-layer normalisation
  throws away. Run by `build.py`'s audit stage.
- **`vision3_granularity.py`** — the re-runnable audit trail for the four adopted σ(D)
  triples. Reads the PDFs, drives `dashtrace`'s public API, writes the coloured overlays and
  fails loudly if it stops reproducing the adopted numbers. Run it after any `dashtrace`
  change.
