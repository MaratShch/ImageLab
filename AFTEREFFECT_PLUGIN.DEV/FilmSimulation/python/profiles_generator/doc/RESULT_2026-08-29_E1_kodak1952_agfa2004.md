Printed p7 carries **Optima 100** beside 200 and 400. The corpus already held a spectral set for
`AGFA_OPTIMA_100`, traced on 2026-08-02 from a **raster** page of the older 1998 *Range of Films*
brochure. Reading the same film off a **vector** page is a cross-check nothing else in the corpus

| layer | 1998 raster reading (stored) | 2004 vector reading |
|---|---:|---:|
| blue | peak **470 nm** | peak **470 nm** |

⚠ **650 nm is where the vector page draws the shoulder, not the peak.** The two readings agree

products. The vector set is adopted, from the better source, and the disagreement is left visible

## 2. Kodak 1952 — what is printed, what is traced, and what is refused

`KODAK/kodak-films-5.pdf` = *Kodak Films*, Data Book, Fifth Edition, Eastman Kodak Company, 1952,
72 pages, plate codes `5-51` and `8-52`. PDF page = printed page + 2 throughout.

Each of the four data sheets prints a **characteristic-curve family of five curves, each labelled
in text with its development time and its gamma.** Those twenty pairs are the data and need no
tracing. `kodak_1952_curves.py` traces all twenty off the raster and measures each slope, as the
check.

| stock | PDF p | developer | printed (min, γ) | measured, % from printed |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| VERICHROME | 35 | **D-76** | 10/.60 14/.70 19/.80 27/.90 36/1.00 | +1.7 +3.1 +1.7 +1.1 +1.4 |
| TRI-X SHEET | 51 | DK-50 | 4/.60 6/.70 8½/.80 12/.90 19/1.00 | −0.1 −1.4 −1.4 −0.7 −1.4 |
| PANATOMIC-X SHEET | 59 | DK-50 | 3/.60 4/.70 5/.80 6/.90 7/1.00 | +0.1 −1.4 −1.6 +1.4 −4.3 |
| ORTHO-X SHEET | 61 | DK-50 | 4½/.60 6½/.70 9/.80 12/.90 16/1.00 | −1.2 −1.5 −1.4 −2.6 −1.9 |

**Eighteen of twenty within 2 %, all twenty within 4.3 %**, and monotone in development time on
every sheet. ⚠ The association of a label to a curve is by **contrast order** — nothing in the
raster links them — so the numbers agreeing is a free test, which it need not have passed.

⚠ **THE ESTIMATOR IS PART OF THE RESULT, AND THE OBVIOUS ONE IS WRONG HERE BY 5 %.**
`kodak_time_gamma.py` fits the H-1-5222 curves over a fixed net-density window of 0.3–1.2 and that
reproduces Kodak's own figures. The same window on these 1952 engravings returns **0.931** where the
sheet prints **1.00**, because the 1952 curves have a far longer toe and 0.3–1.2 above fog is still
inside it. Gamma is the **maximum straight-line slope**; fitting the steepest 0.6-decade chord
instead returns **0.9992**. The window was not tuned to taste — it was replaced with the definition.

### 2.1 The gamma actually stored, and why the interpolation is safe

Each sheet also prints its own recommended development. The stored condition is the **intermittent
(tank) column at 68 F**, because that is what the curve family's caption says it was drawn under.

| stock | condition | γ before | γ after | Δ | how |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| VERICHROME | D-76 **16 min** | 0.780 | **0.744** | −4.6 % | between printed 14 min (.70) and 19 min (.80) |
| PANATOMIC-X SHEET | DK-50 **5½ min** | 0.700 | **0.852** | +21.7 % | between 5 (.80) and 6 (.90) — the exact midpoint |
| TRI-X SHEET | DK-50 **9½ min** (Commercial) | 0.680 | **0.832** | +22.4 % | between 8½ (.80) and 12 (.90) |
| ORTHO-X SHEET | DK-50 **9 min** (Commercial) | 0.720 | **0.800** | +11.1 % | ⚠ **no interpolation at all — 9 min is a printed label** |

⚠ **THE INTERPOLATION MODEL CANNOT MATTER, WHICH IS WHY THIS IS NOT A GUESS.** Every recommended
time falls between two *adjacent* printed curves, so linear-in-time and linear-in-log-time bracket
the answer within **0.004 gamma** — smaller than the trace's own scatter. Both are computed and
printed by the audit rather than one being chosen silently. Independently, the Tri-X sheet's
**Time-Gamma inset** was traced as a worked check and reads its DK-50 curve at **0.827** at 9½ min
against the interpolated 0.829–0.832: agreement to 0.005, from a different plot on the same page.

⚠ Tri-X and Ortho-X each print **two** official rows. The Commercial row is stored; the Portrait
row (7 min ≈ 0.73, and 6 min ≈ 0.69) is not lost — it is one of the five points in each profile's
`ProcessingFamily`.

### 2.2 A negative result: base+fog is not readable from these plots, and `dmin` is unchanged

Schema v13 added `DevelopmentPoint.base_fog` because H-1-5222's curves each reach a flat left
plateau. ⚠ **These do not.** At the leftmost drawn column the Tri-X family is still climbing and
still separated — 0.213 / 0.183 / 0.158 / 0.122 / 0.102 — with no plateau to take a median over.
So `base_fog` is **0 on all twenty points**, which is the schema's *"not stated by the source"*, and
it is a reading of the document rather than an omission.

What each plot *does* draw is a separate horizontal **"Base Density"** line. Traced:

| stock | base density (support alone) | stored `dmin` (base+fog, estimate) |
|---|---:|---:|
| VERICHROME | 0.064 | 0.24 |
| PANATOMIC-X SHEET | 0.050 | 0.18 |
| TRI-X SHEET | 0.080 | 0.20 |
| ORTHO-X SHEET | 0.109 | 0.19 |

⚠ **THAT IS BASE WITHOUT FOG AND IT WOULD BE A CATEGORY ERROR TO STORE IT AS `dmin`.** It bounds
`dmin` from below and nothing more. All four stored estimates clear their bound, which is a real
check that passes; all four stay estimates, and the measured bound is now recorded beside each.

## 3. Agfa 2004 — the vector half

Every stroke on the sheet is the same near-black; the three layers are separated by **dash array**,
the same machine-readable legend `agfa_vista.py` reads on the sister Vista sheet. ⚠ The dash
*lengths* differ between the two sheets (3.402/.851 here against 3.159/.79 there), so
`agfa_2004_curves.py` keys on the **number of entries** in the array rather than the literal string,
and then checks the result two independent ways: against the panel's printed **Blue / Green / Red**
words, and against the physical requirement that the peaks **ascend b < g < r**.

Calibration comes from the sheet's own text layer — real text on a Distiller PDF, so the axis fits
land at **0.00 nm residual** on the spectral panels.

⚠ **THREE SEPARATE LABEL-WINDOW TRAPS HAD TO BE CLOSED, AND ONE OF THEM COST 2.65 DECADES.** A
rectangular window cannot separate these axes: on the colour-density panel the ordinate's "0" is
printed at y 562.5 and the abscissa's row at y 567.6 — 5 pt apart, at almost the same x as the
abscissa's leading minus sign. The reader now keeps only the largest **co-linear group** of numeric
labels, which is decided by count (an axis row is six labels sharing a coordinate; the stray is
alone). The same trap appeared on the sharpness panel in mirror image, where "10 %" on the ordinate
sits inside the abscissa's label row and threw that log fit out by 0.54 decades.

### 3.1 Colour density curves → `ToneCurve`

Six-parameter softplus fits, **rms 0.005–0.016 D**, each one cross-checked against an independent
steepest-chord slope (agreement 0.01–0.04).

| stock | `dmin` r/g/b before | after | `gamma` r/g/b before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AGFA_PORTRAIT_160 | 0.20 / 0.62 / 1.02 | **0.232 / 0.574 / 0.724** | 0.565 / 0.575 / 0.585 | **0.593 / 0.647 / 0.744** |
| AGFA_OPTIMA_100 | 0.20 / 0.62 / 1.02 | **0.265 / 0.676 / 0.918** | 0.600 / 0.610 / 0.620 | **0.640 / 0.645 / 0.691** |
| AGFA_OPTIMA_200 | 0.20 / 0.62 / 1.02 | **0.250 / 0.644 / 0.815** | 0.605 / 0.615 / 0.625 | **0.576 / 0.571 / 0.614** |
| AGFA_OPTIMA_400 | 0.21 / 0.63 / 1.04 | **0.406 / 0.796 / 1.073** | 0.610 / 0.620 / 0.630 | **0.633 / 0.652 / 0.701** |

Two things are worth pulling out of that table. The estimated dmin triple (0.20/0.62/1.02) was a
**good** guess for the Optima 100/200 mask and a poor one at the ends — Portrait 160's blue measures
0.724 against the estimated 1.02, and Optima 400's measures 1.073. And the fitted toe and shoulder
land at **−1.46…−1.59 / +1.66…+1.79** on the Optima columns, against the `_neg` family defaults of
−1.55 / +1.75: an independent confirmation that the family's default *shape* was well chosen even
where its dmin was not.

⚠ **THE ABSCISSA WAS RE-ANCHORED, AND THAT PART IS CONVENTION.** Agfa plot **absolute** exposure,
lg (Lx·s) from −4.0 to +1.0; `ToneCurve.x` is relative to this corpus's mid-grey anchor, and no
document connects the two. dmin, gamma, the toe-to-shoulder **span** and both softnesses are
measured; only the origin is carried, by placing the straight line's midpoint at +0.10 where the
`_neg` defaults put it.

⚠ **AND THE SHOULDER SOFTNESS HAD TO BE CONSTRAINED, WHICH IS THE MOST INSTRUCTIVE FAILURE IN THIS
ITEM.** `ToneCurve`'s own docstring requires `shoulder_k ≤ 1.4 × toe_k`. An unconstrained fit walked
straight past it: Portrait 160's red came back at **3.0×** with rms 0.005 D — a *good* fit to the
drawn curve — and a `gamma` of **1.018**, which that curve does not have. When the two softplus
ramps overlap that heavily the `gamma` **parameter stops being the observable slope**, and the number
would have been stored against a definition it no longer met. Fitting the *ratio* inside the
documented band removes the degeneracy; the steepest-chord check then catches any recurrence.

### 3.2 Spectral sensitivity → three new sets, one replaced

`AGFA_PORTRAIT_160`, `AGFA_OPTIMA_200` and `AGFA_OPTIMA_400` had **no** spectral data; they now
carry vector-traced sets on the corpus's 380 nm / 10 nm grid. `AGFA_OPTIMA_100`'s was replaced (see
§1). The carrier census goes **73 → 76**.

Peaks, b/g/r: Portrait 160 **420 / 550 / 650**, Optima 100 **470 / 550 / 620**, Optima 200
**470 / 550 / 620**, Optima 400 **470 / 560 / 610**.

⚠ Normalisation is done **on the sampled grid, not on the drawn curve**: the true peak generally
falls between two 10 nm samples, so subtracting the curve's own maximum leaves the largest stored
sample slightly negative — −0.05 on Optima 100's red — and `SpectralSensitivity.validate` requires
exactly 0.0. The shift is a few hundredths of a decade and is a normalisation, not a measurement.

### 3.3 Sharpness → adjacency adopted, f50 **refused**

The Sharpness panel plots *"Transfer factor (%)"* against *"Lines per mm"*, and ⚠ **the curve peaks
above 100 %** — 109 to 114 % on these four films. A true MTF is 1.0 at zero frequency by definition,
so what Agfa draw is an adjacency-enhanced response, closer to a printed CTF, and the overshoot is a
direct reading of the adjacency effect.

| stock | peak transfer | `adjacency` before | after | f50 on the sheet's own axis |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AGFA_PORTRAIT_160 | 109 % | 0.09 | **0.090** | 38.4 lines/mm |
| AGFA_OPTIMA_100 | 114 % | 0.09 | **0.135** | 46.2 |
| AGFA_OPTIMA_200 | 113 % | 0.09 | **0.128** | 50.8 |
| AGFA_OPTIMA_400 | 109 % | 0.08 | **0.094** | 50.3 |

The peak is a **ratio** and carries no unit, so it can be adopted whatever the abscissa means —
and Portrait 160's estimate of 0.09 turns out to have been exactly right.

⚠ **f50 IS NOT ADOPTED, AND THAT IS A DECISION RATHER THAN AN OVERSIGHT.** `MTFSpec.f50` is in
**cycles/mm**; Agfa's abscissa says **"Lines per mm"**, and whether that means line pairs is an open
question in this corpus — it is **queue item G6**, raised on 2026-08-19 against Gevacolor 682's
identically worded axis. A factor of two is not a rounding difference. The four readings are filed
as evidence on G6 and the f50 fields stay estimates until it is settled. ⚠ Note also that the
readings do **not** rank the way the printed resolving powers do (Optima 100 reads 46.2 against
Optima 200's 50.8, while their published resolving powers are 140 and 130 lines/mm at 1000:1) —
which is one more reason not to store them before the axis is understood.

### 3.4 Not adoptable: the Spectral density panel

It plots **"Medium density"** and **"Minimum density"** — the film's total transmission at two
exposure levels — and not the three separated dye densities. `SpectralDyeDensity` wants cyan,
magenta and yellow; two aggregate curves cannot be split into three. `agfa_vista.py` reached the
same conclusion on the sister sheet, and this repeats it rather than quietly dropping the panel.

Reciprocity was checked and **needed nothing**: the corpus's Schwarzschild exponents for these four
stocks were already derived from this sheet's own p6 correction table.
