## 2. `KODAK_VISION2_50D_5201` — the first stock measured end to end from one sheet

**Source:** Kodak **H-1-5201**, CAT 122 9749, **New 10-2005**, 4 pp, `PDF/PROFILES/KODAK/Kodak
VISION2 50D 5201.pdf` (PDF title `62084-Broch.pdf`). Page 3 carries five vector panels and zero
embedded images.

⚠ **The sheet prints no scalar for grain, sharpness or gamma.** On grain it says only *"the
measured granularity is exceptionally low"*. Every number below is measured off the curves; nothing
is transferred from a sibling.

### 2.1 What was printed as text, and adopted verbatim

EI **50** daylight (5500 K) · ECN-2 · acetate safety base **with rem-jet** · reciprocity none from
1/1000 s to **1 s**, +1/3 stop and a CC10R at 10 s · 35 mm SP718 and 16 mm SP455/445/457 · storage
13 °C, −18 °C beyond 6 months.

⚠ **The tungsten 12 is not stored.** It exists only as *"12 (with KODAK WRATTEN Gelatin Filter No.
80A)"* — a filter factor. Corroboration that 50 is the film's own index: Metal Halide H.M.I. and
VITALITE are both printed at **50 with no filter**.

⚠ **The reciprocity onset is 1 s here, not 1/10 s.** Its VISION2 sibling 5205 prints 1/10; taking
the family value would have claimed a failure a decade earlier than Kodak prints for this stock.

### 2.2 Characteristic curves — and why the panel labelled "SENSITOMETRIC CURVES" was refused

**This is the substantive methodological finding of the session, and it is now method rule 22.**

At brochure scale, H-1-5201's sensitometric panel draws the **red and green records as
five-segment polylines — six vertices each** — spanning only −6…+6 camera stops (±1.8 decades),
with **neither dmin nor dmax on the plot**. Fitting the six-parameter softplus to six points
returned:

```
R  ToneCurve(0.1431, 0.5237, -1.0624, 0.1496, 1.7266, 0.2089)   rms 0.0004 D
```

An rms of 0.0004 D over six points and six free parameters is an **interpolation wearing a
measurement's error bar**. `shoulder_x` came out 1.73 with no shoulder anywhere in the data.

The curves actually used are the ones Kodak draws **densely, inside the granularity panel on the
same page**: 100 / 125 / 121 samples per record across 4.1 decades, reaching the toe plateau and
the shoulder. The coarse panel is then asked for **exactly one number** — the abscissa origin,
which the granularity panel does not state (*"RELATIVE LOG EXPOSURE"*) and the sensitometric panel
does, in words: *"'0' on the x-axis represents normal exposure of an 18-percent gray card"*.
**Six points per record is ample for one parameter and hopeless for six.**

| record | adopted | rms | worst | dmax | latitude |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | `ToneCurve(0.1662, 0.5129, -0.9426, 0.1289, 1.8751, 0.1804)` | 0.0053 D | 0.017 D | 1.61 | 9.4 stops |
| G | `ToneCurve(0.6240, 0.5945, -0.9330, 0.1412, 1.9680, 0.1060)` | 0.0065 D | 0.013 D | 2.35 | 9.6 stops |
| B | `ToneCurve(1.0149, 0.5954, -0.9741, 0.1997, 2.1490, 0.2773)` | 0.0064 D | 0.016 D | 2.87 | 10.4 stops |

Shift **+1.9932 decades**, fitted over all three records on their **straight-line sections only** —
a six-vertex polyline is a set of *chords*, so matching in the toe and shoulder measures the chord
error rather than the offset. Restricting to D between dmin+0.3 and dmin+1.2 cut the residual from
0.048 to 0.026 D; using every vertex instead would shift by 0.017 decade (**0.06 stop**), so the
choice is reported, not agonised over.

**Two independent checks the fit had to pass:**

* the sheet prints **two abscissae** — Camera Stops gives 24.70 pt/decade and the LOG EXPOSURE
  (lux-seconds) ladder along the top gives 24.47 pt/decade, **0.9 % apart**. A stops axis mis-read
  by a factor of two is otherwise a plausible curve with half the latitude and no other symptom.
* 0 camera stops reads **log H = −0.709 lux-seconds** on the sheet's own absolute ladder. For an
  EI 50 film, mid-grey sits at H ≈ 10/S = 0.2 lux-s, i.e. log H = **−0.70**. The panel is
  internally consistent with the printed speed to 0.01 decade.

⚠ **The fitted toe_x (−0.93…−0.97) does not match the −1.42…−1.58 stored on the VISION2 siblings,
and that is a statement about the siblings.** 5217/5218/5205 all carry toe_k **0.300** and
shoulder_k **0.420** in all three channels — the signature of hand-set numbers. 5201's are the
family's first measured pair. What *does* agree is everything on an absolute scale: measured gamma
0.513 / 0.595 / 0.595 against 5218's 0.56 / 0.58 / 0.598, and dmin 0.166 / 0.624 / 1.015 against
0.22 / 0.66 / 1.06. `verify.py` now asserts 5201's softnesses are **not** the family pair, so a
tidy-up that rewrites the entry with `_neg()` fails loudly.

⚠ **Red's `shoulder_k` is pinned at exactly 1.4 × `toe_k`.** The free fit wanted 1.4004× — the
optimiser sitting on the project's monotonicity constraint, because `fit_tonecurve` enforces it
with a soft penalty. Imposing it exactly cost **0.0000 D** of fit.

### 2.3 Grain — a ninth vector sheet, and the flattest σ(D) in the corpus

`granularity_vector.py` grew from 8 sheets to **9**, and this is the first one that carries a stock
the database did not already hold.

**Level, read at NET 1.0** — the convention Kodak prints on the sheets that carry a number (5248 p1,
5222 p1) — all three records at the single exposure point where green reaches its own net 1.0:

| | r | g | b | blue ÷ green |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one exposure point (adopted) | 4.36 | **4.51** | 9.63 | 2.14× |
| each record at its own net 1.0 | 4.41 | 4.51 | 9.71 | — |

The two conventions agree to 2 %. Blue at 2.14× green sits inside the 1.9–2.8× measured across the
six negatives re-levelled under C1d, and nowhere near the 1.3× the old tier-2 ladder assumed.

**Shape, green record:** σ×1000 = 2.98 at D 0.621, 5.54 at D 1.0, 4.93 at D 2.349, peaking at 6.65
at D 0.80 → ratios **0.54 / 1.00 / 0.89 with an interior maximum of only 1.20×**.

⚠ **1.20× is the flattest σ(D) in the corpus** — the other six colour negatives run 1.38–1.62×.
This is what "exceptionally low granularity" looks like in the *shape* as well as the level, and it
means 5201's grain differs from its siblings **in kind**: much less shadow-to-midtone modulation.

⚠ The toe anchor sits on a plateau (σ spans 2.97–4.03 within 0.02 D of it), so 0.54 is the low end
of an ill-posed anchor — as on five of the other eight sheets.

### 2.4 MTF — the first per-record measurement in the database

| record | f50 (cycles/mm) | overshoot | peak at | rolloff q | vs Gaussian |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | 32.1 | +0.108 ⚠ | 2.5 (panel edge) | 2.77 | 2.3× better |
| G | **49.7** | +0.157 | 10.7 | **3.23** | 1.3× better |
| B | 55.5 | +0.142 | 12.7 | 3.42 | 1.3× better |

**f50 rises red → green → blue.** That is the layer order `MTFSpec`'s docstring predicts — blue on
top, red at the bottom under the most scattered light — and this is the **first direct confirmation
of it in this database**. Until now every colour stock's three f50 values were one estimate scaled
by that stored ratio; these are three measurements.

⚠ **The red overshoot is a lower bound**: the red curve is still rising at the panel's left edge
(2.5 cycles/mm), so its true peak is off the plot. The stored `adjacency` is green's 0.157; the
spread is recorded rather than averaged.

⚠ **`adjacency_um` contradicts this sheet too.** Green's overshoot peaks at 10.7 cycles/mm, a
spatial scale of ~93 µm, against a stored 16.0. Third stock, same direction. Left alone — **C2c**.

### 2.5 What was deliberately left empty

Both panels are on the sheet as vector art and **neither was guessed nor transferred**:

* **dye density** — the panel draws **five** traces (Midscale Neutral, Cyan, Magenta, Yellow,
  Minimum Density) and states *"Cyan, Magenta, and Yellow Dye Curves are peak-normalized"*.
  `dye_density.py`'s family classifier handles 3 dyes, or 3 dyes + neutral, not 3 + neutral + dmin,
  and returns *"no curve set matched either normalisation family"*. Widening it blind would risk
  the 11 sheets it already reproduces. → **C9**
* **spectral sensitivity** — three curves in R/G/B ink over 250–750 nm with the criterion printed,
  but no vector extractor exists for that panel type. → **C10**

`verify.py` asserts both stay **unsourced**, and that the provenance names C9 and C10.

## 3. C6 — `FUJI_SUPER_F125_8532`, and a sharpness trap avoided

**Source:** Fuji product sheet, 35 mm type **8532** / 16 mm 8632, `PDF/PROFILES/FUJI/
Fujifilm-Super-F-125-8532-35mm-Motion-Picture-Film.pdf`. ⚠ **No printed date** — the only date
evidence is the PDF's own creation stamp, **2001-08-24**, and the text calls it *"the new Fujifilm
F-125"*, so the sheet is launch material and the era is bounded below, not dated.

Adopted `[T1]` from print: EI **125** at 3200 K with no filter; **rms 3.0**; reciprocity none
1/1000–1/10 s, +1/3 stop at 1 s; Status M densitometry, exposure 3200 K for 1/50 s through a Fuji
SC-41; the spectral criterion; MR code edge marking with mark FN32.

⚠ **Fuji prints the granularity convention explicitly** — *"1000 times the data obtained from the
measurement taken at a visual diffuse density 1.0 above the minimum density; a 48 µm diameter
aperture used"*. **"1.0 above the minimum density" is net 1.0**, this database's reference since
C1b, so 3.0 needs no conversion. First Fuji sheet in the corpus to print it rather than leave it
to be inferred. It is also a large claim: 8530 carries 5.4 (an estimate), so Fuji claims **0.56×**
the granularity of the stock it replaced — consistent with the SUFG flat-hexagonal grain claim, and
adopted as printed rather than reconciled with the estimate it contradicts.

⚠ **The sheet's sharpness panel is a CONTRAST TRANSFER FUNCTION, not an MTF.** Its own footnote:
*"Spatial frequency attenuation characteristic of amplitude relative to **rectangular** wave chart"*.
Square-wave response runs above the sine-wave MTF by up to 4/π at low frequency, so reading f50 off
it would overstate sharpness. **Not read.** 8530's measured 42.0 c/mm (Honjo 1989, green record) is
transferred with the same flanking ratio, and `verify.py` asserts 8532 is **not** flagged
`mtf_measured` and that the word *"rectangular"* survives in its provenance — so nobody
"finishes the job" by flagging it.

⚠ **The curves are a flagged `[T2]` transfer from 8530 and the sheet says the transfer is wrong in
a known direction**: *"The new film's more linear response curve assures greater shadow detail"* and
*"an outstandingly long, smooth tonal scale, further helping to increase exposure latitude"*. So the
real 8532 toe is straighter and its latitude wider than the stored numbers say. Tier **2**: it has a
real datasheet (not 3) but its core curves are transferred (not 1). → **C11** traces all four
panels.

⚠ Fuji states the reciprocity failure is **achromatic** — *"does not need lens opening adjustment
nor filtration"* — so unlike the Kodak entries this one carries **no channel spread**
(p = 0.90 / 0.90 / 0.90, onset 1/10 s). Inventing a red offset would contradict the source, and a
guard now says so.

## 4. C5 — `EASTMAN_5247_1983` re-tiered 3 → 1

The profile carried **no tier tag at all**, so it read as tier 3 while owning Kodak **TI0835
(Revised 6-93)** outright: printed EI 125/22 tungsten and 80/20 daylight, clear acetate base with
rem-jet, ECN-2, storage and darkroom conditions, the reciprocity table, *"rms Granularity: less
than 5"*, and a spectral set digitised from plate TI0835C.

Re-tiered to **1** with the tag `[T1/T2]`, because its three tone curves are hand-fitted and the
split says so. Promoting the whole profile would have overstated the curves.

⚠ **A mixed tag does not resolve itself.** `_provenance_for`'s regex accepts only a bare
`[T1]`/`[T2]`/`[T3]`, so `[T1/T2]` falls through to `_UNTAGGED_TIER` — and if it is not listed
there, silently to **3**. That is exactly what has happened to `KODAK_VISION2_500T_5218` and
`_200T_5217` (`[T1/T3]`, both sitting at tier 3 to this day). Both new mixed-tag entries are listed
explicitly and a guard pins them.

## 5. C2b — six more MTF curves, and q tracks layer depth rather than film

C2 adopted `1/(1+(f/f50)^q)` on **one** curve at q = 1.84 and its own result entry called that the
weakest part of the choice. C2b asks whether more curves agree.

| curve | q | rms | Gaussian rms | power law better by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PLUS-X 5231 (B&W) | 1.84 | 0.0376 | 0.0878 | 2.3× |
| 5201 R | 2.77 | 0.0268 | 0.0624 | 2.3× |
| 5201 G | 3.23 | 0.0845 | 0.1084 | 1.3× |
| 5201 B | 3.42 | 0.0841 | 0.1116 | 1.3× |
| **5274 R** | **1.89** | **0.0149** | 0.0619 | **4.2×** |
| 5274 G | 2.94 | 0.1083 | 0.1196 | 1.1× |
| 5274 B | 3.38 | 0.1318 | 0.1523 | 1.2× |

**The power law wins all seven — and q is not a family constant.** 1.84 against 3.42 is not scatter
around a shared value. But the pattern is not noise either: **both red records land at 1.84–1.89 and
both blue records at 3.38–3.42**, on two different stocks a decade apart. q may be a property of
DEPTH IN THE LAYER STACK rather than of the film, which is a sharper hypothesis than C2b started
with and is testable with two or three more colour sheets. `verify.py` now pins the *disagreement*, so a future "let's just use
1.84 everywhere" simplification fails loudly instead of looking tidy.

⚠ **The 8 cycles/mm cut C2 used is wrong on this stock.** It came from 5231, whose overshoot peaks
at 4.7 c/mm. 5201's green peaks at **10.7** and its blue at **12.7**, so a fixed 8 leaves the
adjacency lift inside the fitted band — the power law then scores rms 0.095, a number about fitting
a rolloff through an overshoot rather than about the carrier. `mtf_vector.py` now cuts at each
record's own overshoot peak (`max(8, f_peak)`), which leaves 5231's published numbers untouched.

## 7. The 5274 MTF — traced because of the re-upload, and the estimating rule is wrong in red

`V200T.pdf` is byte-identical to `5274.pdf`, so it added no document. It did make someone look at
the sheet again, and **H-1-5274 p3's modulation-transfer panel had never been traced** — only its
granularity panel had, under C1c.

⚠ **This sheet identifies its curves by PRINTED LETTER, not by ink.** All three are black; R / G / B
are set inside the frame's right edge. The 2005 brochures state the record in colour. So a
grey-ink sheet is not necessarily a one-curve sheet — which is exactly what `mtf_vector`'s old
"all grey → take the thickest path" branch assumed.

| record | stored (estimate) | **measured** | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | 56.0 | **35.4** | stored is **1.58× too sharp** |
| G | 64.0 | **68.8** | 0.93× — agrees |
| B | 72.0 | **74.0** | 0.97× — agrees |
| adjacency | 0.09 | 0.162 (green) | — |

**Green and blue confirm the stored estimates; red does not, and the reason is structural.** The
estimating rule takes one number and scales it by a fixed layer-order ratio. Both stocks measured
today say the real red deficit is much larger than that ratio allows: 5274's measured red:blue is
0.48 and 5201's is 0.58, where the stored rule assumes about 0.78. **That is a defect in the rule,
not in one profile** — it affects every colour stock whose f50 triple is an estimate.

Rolloff, for C2b: **q = 1.89 / 2.94 / 3.38**, with red fitting at rms 0.0149 and beating the
Gaussian **4.2×** — the best carrier fit yet measured. Red's q of 1.89 lands almost exactly on
PLUS-X's 1.84.

⚠ **Measured, pinned in `mtf_vector.EXPECTED`, and NOT written into the profile.** Changing 5274's
sharpness changes an existing stock, which needs an owner yes/no. Pinning it means the reading is
re-derived on every build and cannot drift while the decision waits — the same treatment the 5219
brochure cross-check gets. Queue **C13**.

⚠ One more label defect found here: the response axis prints 1 2 3 5 7 10 20 30 50 70 100 **150**,
and "150" sits 7.6 pt off the line the other eleven define to 0.2 % — the axis is clipped at the
frame top and the label was set at the frame edge. `logfit` gained the same outlier rejection
`granularity_vector.fit()` has. 5231 and 5201 drop nothing.

## 8. `Kodak Ektar 125` — a real gap, and a document that cannot fill it

**Source:** *PHOTOgraphic*, **September 1989**, pp. 80–82, "EKTAR" by **Jack and Sue Drafahl** —
three raster pages with an OCR text layer. A **magazine review**, not a datasheet.

**No profile was created**, and this is the reason: the article contains **no sensitometry at all**
— no rms granularity, no gamma or contrast index, no dmin/dmax, no MTF or resolving power, no
spectral data, no reciprocity, no processing table. Building `KODAK_EKTAR_125` from it would mean
inventing every stored number.

What it **does** document, and what is now on file as a research lead:

* Ektar 125 and Ektar 25 Professional were **released in 1989**, joining Ektar 25 and Ektar 1000;
* **eleven layers**, deliberately re-arranged relative to Ektar 25;
* **two blue layers** (one fast, one slow), *"slightly thicker than the single blue, slow layer
  found in Ektar 25"*;
* **an extra interlayer between the two green layers**, restricting coupler migration — the
  article attributes the sharpness gain to it;
* *"For the first time, a new type of magenta color coupler"* that raises the T-grain emulsion's
  speed;
* **two red layers with DIAR couplers**, as in Ektar 25, inhibiting dye formation to raise
  saturation;
* wider exposure latitude than Ektar 25.

That is a complete `LayerStack` description and a `CouplerSpec` rationale for a stock that has no
curves — enough to populate two fields *if a profile ever exists*, and not enough to justify one.
Recorded in `NotFound.md` with the Kodak publication to look for. Queue **C14**.

⚠ The database holds `KODAK_EKTAR_100` (E-4046, 2008/2016), a **different, later** film. Ektar 125
(1989–1994) remains unrepresented.

## 9. The fifth file — VISION3 DI 2254/5254, and the first real `AgingSpec` data in the project

**Source:** *"BRINGING ENHANCED PERFORMANCE TO THE DIGITAL WORKFLOW"*, KODAK VISION3 Color Digital
Intermediate Film **2254/5254**, 2 pp, vector. Not one of the four named files.

⚠ **CATALOGUE-NUMBER COLLISION, and it is a live one.** The database holds `EASTMAN_5254_1968` — a
1968 ECN **camera negative**. This document is a **digital-intermediate recording film** of the same
number, forty years later. Added to the standing hazards list beside the 5294/5285 case.

No profile was added: a DI recording film is a different animal from a camera negative (the sheet
says *"designed for recording ONLY"* and that *"the contrast and speed positions of the three color
records are not aligned to produce an optimal image"*), and its full technical sheet
(`KODAK-VISION3-2254-technical-information.pdf`) is already in the corpus unread.

**What is genuinely new is the dye-stability table**, and it is the first source-backed data for
`AgingSpec` — a struct that has been all zeros for every stock in the file since it was written:

| record | years to 0.10 density loss, 21 °C | at 7 °C |
|---|---|---|
| neutral R / G / B at D 1.0 | >100 / >100 / >100 | >100 |
| colour separations C / M / **Y** | >100 / >100 / **86** | >100 |
| D-min gain R / G / **B** | >100 / >100 / **77** | >100 |

Yellow fades first and blue D-min rises first — which is the mechanism `AgingSpec`'s docstring
describes in prose ("magenta usually survives longest") now attached to a number, from
high-temperature Arrhenius testing. Also printed: 2254 is **ESTAR** base with a conductive
anti-static layer and a polymeric scratch-resistant backing; 5254 is acetate with rem-jet;
*"up to 1½ stops greater exposure sensitivity than KODAK VISION Color Intermediate Film
2242/5242"*; latent-image keeping *"less than one printer light shift ... up to three days"*.
Queue **C15**.
