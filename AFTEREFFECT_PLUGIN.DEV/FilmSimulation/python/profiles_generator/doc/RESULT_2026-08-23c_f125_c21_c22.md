## 1. F-125 — a wrong sentence, and what was under it

### What the owner caught

`doc/NotFound.md` §0.2.1 said, of `FUJI_F125_8530` / `_8630`:

> no Fuji F-125 document existed anywhere in the corpus

That is false as written. `PDF/PROFILES/FUJI/Fujifilm-Super-F-125-8532-35mm-Motion-Picture-Film.pdf`
is a complete two-page Fuji product sheet, **Ref. No. KB-913E (SK·99·05·DT·MW), ©1999**, and its
own printed title is **FUJICOLOR NEGATIVE FILM F-125** — the same product designation. It had
been on disk since 2026-08-18.

### Root cause, which matters more than the correction

Two habits combined, and only one of them was about F-125.

1. **The type number was read as the identity.** "8530 ≠ 8532, therefore not an F-125 document."
   But the *product name* is what a reader looking for "Fuji F-125 documentation" searches for,
   and the sheet carries it.

2. **The sheet's typography is outlined vector art, not text.** `get_text()` on page 1 returns no
   "F-125", no "KB-913E", no "©1999" — the footer, the product name and the FUJIFILM logotype are
   all curves. A text-layer reading of that file genuinely looks like a sheet with no product name
   and no date, which is also how this project came to record, in `NotFound.md` and in the profile
   itself, that the sheet carries "no printed date anywhere" and to date the document from its PDF
   creation stamp instead.

**Rule adopted.** On a sheet whose typography is outlined, *"not in the text layer" is not "not
printed"* — render the page and look. One 110 dpi render of page 1 shows the whole footer.

### What is actually missing, stated at the right width

No sheet for **type 8530 / 8630**. 8532/8632 is the successor generation of the same product
line and Fuji's own page says so — "Announcing the new Fujifilm F-125", "the newly upgraded
F-125", new **SUFG** flat hexagonal grain "just 1/3 the size of conventional grain", new
**Two-Stage Timing DIR Couplers**, and "a more linear response curve". A sheet documenting a
different emulsion under the same trade name cannot be lifted onto 8530/8630 without asserting
the two are the same film, which that page explicitly denies. The acquisition target is
unchanged; only the claim about the corpus was wrong.

The measured numbers make the generation gap concrete rather than rhetorical: 8532 reads rms
**3.0** against 8530's estimated 5.4, and Dmin **0.19 / 0.44 / 0.78** against the
0.20 / 0.60 / 0.98 that the old transfer — in the other direction — had put on 8532.

### Harvested and adopted

Everything printed or plotted on the sheet is now in the database. The three panels that could be
traced were traced; the fourth cannot be stored for a schema reason, not a source reason.

| Panel | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Characteristic curves (3 layers) | **traced**, rms 0.0090 / 0.0077 / 0.0050 D, worst point 0.033 / 0.023 / 0.019 D |
| Contrast transfer function | **converted**, sine f50 **32.07 c/mm** |
| Spectral sensitivity (3 layers) | **traced**, peaks 469 / 553 / 645 nm |
| Spectral density (neutral + Dmin) | read, **not storable** — see below |

**Curves.** The three records are stroked PDF paths, so their coordinates are exact and only the
axis calibration is fitted. Fitted against the project's softplus `ToneCurve`:

```
r  ToneCurve(0.1903, 0.4943, -1.6820, 0.0850, 2.1000, 0.1190)
g  ToneCurve(0.4360, 0.5547, -1.6833, 0.1951, 2.1000, 0.2731)
b  ToneCurve(0.7825, 0.5773, -1.6355, 0.1732, 2.1000, 0.2424)
```

⚠ **The shoulder is held, not fitted, and that is a real limit of the sheet.** The drawn curve
stops at x = +0.87 (mid grey +2.9 stops) and is still climbing, so the panel contains no
shoulder at all. A free six-parameter fit put `shoulder_x` at **0.85 for red** — a knee sitting
exactly on the last drawn point, which would clip red highlights three stops above grey in every
render while reporting an excellent rms. So `shoulder_x` is pinned at 2.10 (the mean of the
traced Kodak stocks) and `shoulder_k` at 1.4·`toe_k`, the project's monotonicity bound. Neither
number is measured on this film, and the profile says so at the field.

**Sharpness.** The panel is headed *Contrast transfer function*, footnoted "amplitude relative to
rectangular wave chart … normalized with the amplitude of a zero frequency" — a **square-wave**
response, which runs above the sine-wave MTF. This project had recorded the conversion as
blocked "without the chart's duty cycle". It is not: a rectangular wave chart is 1:1, and
Coltman's inversion needs only the curve.

```
MTF(f) = (π/4) [ C(f) + C(3f)/3 − C(5f)/5 + C(7f)/7 + C(11f)/11 − C(13f)/13 − C(15f)/15 + … ]
         coefficient  μ(n)·(−1)^((n−1)/2) / n  over odd n
```

⚠ **The sign pattern is not μ(n)/n**, which is the obvious wrong guess and the one made first
here: it produced MTF(2 c/mm) = 0.11 off a CTF of 1.11, which is how the error announced itself.
The published series is +1, +1/3, −1/5, +1/7, +1/11, −1/13, −1/15 …, and that is what
μ(n)·(−1)^((n−1)/2)/n reproduces.

| | 8532 | 8572 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CTF crossing, as printed | 37.78 c/mm | 24.79 c/mm |
| **sine f50, converted** | **32.07 c/mm** | **20.21 c/mm** |
| tail scaled −30 % / +40 % | 30.98 / 33.49 | 19.66 / 20.91 |
| low-frequency overshoot, converted | +9.0 % | +0.9 % |
| rolloff exponent q at the measured f50 | 1.915 (rms 0.017) | 1.700 (rms 0.007) |

The `+C(3f)/3` term reaches past the drawn range, so the tail above 50 c/mm is a power law
fitted to the trace's own `f ≥ 20` section (rms 0.011 log). Scaling that tail by −30 %/+40 %
moves f50 by about 1.3 c/mm, so the result is not an artefact of how the tail is guessed.

Both stocks are flagged `mtf_measured` and both keep `q`: unlike `KODAK_VISION_500T_5279`, whose
+42 %/+55 % printed overshoot cannot be fitted by a carrier that is 1.0 at f = 0, a +9 %
overshoot leaves the power law fitting to rms 0.017 — 3.5× better than the legacy Gaussian on
8532 and 17× better on 8572.

⚠ **Red and blue remain `[T2]`.** The panel is one unlabelled black curve, so it yields one
number. It is assigned to green for the reason Honjo gives — the magenta record sits in the
middle of the pack and dominates the visual response — and red/blue keep each profile's existing
flanking ratios. This is the same method the `FUJI_F125_8530` entry already uses.

⚠ **`adjacency` is measured but is a LOWER BOUND.** 8532's converted MTF peaks at 1.090, so
`adjacency = 0.090`. But the drawn trace begins at 2.04 c/mm *already at its maximum* and stays
flat to 5 c/mm, so the panel does not resolve where the overshoot peaks and the true peak may be
higher. `adjacency_um` therefore stays the transferred 13.0: the magnitude is measured here, the
scale is not.

**Spectral sensitivity.** Adopted on the 380–700 nm / 10 nm grid, peak-normalised. The `-4.00`
entries are Fuji's truncation at the layer crossovers, not a measured floor — the traces stop in
mid-air at −0.43 (blue, 500 nm), −1.49 (green, 590 nm) and −0.41 (red, 660 nm). Nothing was
extrapolated to smooth the step: `FUJI_ETERNA_VIVID_500T_8547` already carries the same
convention from an equivalent Fuji sheet, and extrapolating would be inventing a cut-off slope.

**Spectral density — read, and deliberately not stored.** Fuji plots only "Typical Densities for
a Mid-scale Neutral Subject" and "Minimum Densities" and never separates cyan, magenta and
yellow, while `SpectralDyeDensity.validate()` requires all three. This is a **schema-shape
mismatch, not a missing document**, and `NotFound.md` now files it that way. Both traces are
quoted in the profile comment so the numbers survive. The minimum-density trace is the orange
mask, falling 0.85 → 0.03 D across 400–700 nm; it could ground `base_tint`, but that needs a
band-integration convention no other stock in the file uses, so it is recorded and not adopted.

### ⚠ Two hazards recorded rather than smoothed

**1. The exposure axis on both Super-F sheets is mis-set.** The ten uniformly spaced gridline
labels print, left to right:

```
−4.5  −3.0  −3.5  −2.0  −2.5  −1.0  −1.5   0.0   0.5   1.0
```

which is **not monotonic**. The sheet contradicts itself, so at least four labels are wrong
whatever the truth is, and the same sequence appears character for character on the F-500 8572
sheet — which is what identifies it as a template defect rather than a misread.

The gridlines themselves are exactly uniform (0.5 decade each), so only the **origin** was in
doubt, and it was settled by physics, against two candidate readings:

| Test | first gridline = −4.5 | first gridline = −3.5 | traced Kodak family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fitted `toe_x` | **−1.60 … −1.68** | −0.62 … −0.68 | −1.48 … −1.59 |
| mid-grey green density | **1.37** | 0.82 | 1.41 … 1.44 |

Both tests point the same way and the label-parsimony argument (which reading leaves fewer labels
wrong) points the *other* way, 6 matches against 4 — which is exactly why it was not used to
decide. The adopted reading is **first gridline = −4.5, axis −4.5 … 0.0**, and the six labels
−3.0 −2.0 −1.0 0.0 0.5 1.0 are each +1.0 from the truth.

**Independent cross-check.** With that origin and the project's mid-grey convention (x = 0 at
normal exposure of an 18 % grey card, H = 10/EI lux·s), the two sheets' green speed points sit
**0.577 decades** apart against the **0.602** their printed exposure indices (125 and 500)
demand — 0.08 stop. That agreement is the strongest evidence available that the exposure scale is
read correctly, and it is why tracing the sister sheet was worth doing in the same pass.

**2. The measured 8532 is softer than the 8530 it replaced, and the conflict is left standing.**
32.07 c/mm against Honjo's 42.0, while Fuji's own page sells the film on "dramatically increased
sharpness" from Two-Stage Timing DIR couplers. The two figures are not the same measurement —
Honjo's is a visual-density MTF of a master negative inside a duplication chain, this one a
square-wave response at visual diffuse density 1.0 — and method rule 4 says record the conflict,
never average. `verify.py` now **pins** it, so a later pass cannot quietly nudge 8532 up toward
42 to make the marketing copy come true.

⚠ **Two corrections it forced.** The stored Dmin triple was 0.23 / 0.22 / 0.21 — nearly *equal*

Its f50_g moves **56 → 20.21 c/mm**, which will visibly soften every render of this stock. It is

### Report-generator defect found on the way

`gen_active_profiles.py` decided the MTF column's "documented vs estimated" colour from a keyword
regex (`lp/mm|lines/mm|lin/mm|MTF|resolving power`) over the profile's prose. A stock whose curve
had actually been traced could therefore be printed **in red as the model's own estimate** —
which is what was happening to `KODAK_VISION_500T_5279`, and would have happened to both Fuji
stocks, whose prose says "contrast transfer function" and "Coltman" and never the literal token.
`mtf_measured` is set only by an audit that re-derives the curve from the sheet on every build,
so it is stronger evidence than any keyword match, and it now short-circuits the rule.

## 2. C21 — per-channel halation radii (schema v11)

`HalationSpec` carried a three-lobe kernel with per-channel **gains** but a shared radius triple,
so it could make red *stronger* but not *tighter*. It now carries `radius_scale_r/g/b`, and both
C++ flavours build the halation kernel **once per record** instead of once per frame, with a
per-record resolvability skip so a tight record that falls below a quarter pixel is passed
through while a wider one still blurs.

⚠ **All 159 stocks ship 1.0, and `verify.py` pins them there. That is the finding, not a
shortfall.** The path-length geometry bounds the real per-channel ratio: the base is 100–150 µm
thick against an 11–16 µm emulsion pack, so the difference in return path between the record
nearest the base and the one furthest from it is about **1.1×**. A `LayerStack`-derived set would
therefore *look* measured while moving a render by roughly 1 %. The guard exists precisely to
stop a later pass "finishing the feature" from the layer order. What would fill the fields
honestly is a per-channel edge trace across a specular highlight.

Renders are bit-identical to v10 — the scaling is a multiply by exactly 1.0, exact in every float
format. The **struct grew**, which is why `kSchemaVersion` moved with it: a v10 reader would walk
off the end of every `HalationSpec`.

## 3. C22 — Callier's coefficient as film × scanner geometry

`callier_q` existed on all 159 stocks and was read by nothing. Its scoping was already right —
Q = 1.0 on all 93 colour stocks, 1.25/1.3 on the 66 monochrome ones, because Callier is a
**silver**-scattering effect and a chromogenic dye image has essentially none. What was wrong is
that the field modelled Q as a property of the **film**, when it is a property of film ×
**measuring geometry**: the same negative reads differently on a diffuse LED integrating-sphere
scanner and on a directed halogen condenser.

So the film keeps its scattering and the reader gets its own input, `scanner_specular` /
`AlgoControls::scannerSpecular`, shipped at 0:

```
D_read = dmin + (D − dmin) · (1 + specular · (Q − 1))
```

⚠ **Referenced to dmin, not to zero**, and that is physics rather than convenience: the
scattering scales with developed silver, so clear base carries none of it. Referenced to zero, a
condenser would darken the film base, which no densitometer measures. The `cpp_parity` family
deliberately probes densities **below** dmin, because that is where the two laws differ.

⚠ **THE FACTOR MUST REACH THREE CONSUMERS, AND THIS IS THE PART THAT IS EASY TO GET WRONG.** A
lab that switches to a condenser head **re-times the print** — that is what printer lights are
for. If only the pixel pass sees Callier, the render both steepens the tone scale *and* shifts
mid grey, and the shift is the bigger error:

| Consumer | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `AlgoSolveAnchors` / `solve_anchors` | the print offset that lands mid grey |
| `AlgoNeutralMidDensity` / `neutral_mid_density` | the print chain's own mid-grey reference |
| `AlgoStage12b_Callier` / `callier_density` | the per-pixel pass |

Measured on `EASTMAN_DOUBLE_X_5222` at specular 1: with the pixel pass and the anchor solve
corrected but the print chain's own reference left blind, mid grey moved **+54/255** against a
contrast change of 22 %. One of those is the physics; the other is the laboratory failing to do
its job. With all three agreeing, mid grey holds to **0.2/255** while contrast rises ×1.21–1.23
(mono negative) and ×1.11 (mono reversal), and every colour stock is bit-identical at any
setting.

⚠ **The film half of the product is still unsourced.** The two monochrome values are a class rule
in the generator, not a document. The geometry half is exact. That asymmetry is why the control
ships at 0 rather than at some "typical scanner" value, and what would fix it is one densitometer
specification stating a diffuse-versus-specular ratio for a named emulsion.

## 4. Audits and guards

**`cpp_parity.py` gains a fourth family**, compiled against the plugin's own `AlgoCallier.hpp`:
11448 probes over 159 stocks × 3 channels × 4 specular settings × 6 densities including one below
dmin. Worst disagreement **1.4e-07**.

⚠ **The first tolerance, 1e-9, was too tight, and the reason is worth more than the number.** The
law is three exact operations, so bit agreement looked reasonable — and the run returned 1.43e-07
on `AGFA_APX_100`. The cause is **storage, not arithmetic**: `FilmProfile::callier_q` is a
`float`, so a stored 1.3 reaches C++ as 1.2999999523 while `film_sim` reads the Python double.
At specular 0.6 and 3.0 D above dmin that is 3.0 × 0.6 × 4.8e-8 = 8.6e-8, exactly the residual
observed. The tolerance is now 1e-6, which still leaves six decades of headroom over what a
reference-to-zero bug would produce.

Beyond parity comparison the family asserts what must **move**: exact inertness at specular 0,
inertness on colour at every setting, ≥ 300 rows carrying a non-unit factor, and identity at
dmin. A parity check that only compares would pass perfectly if both sides did nothing.

**`verify.py` 303 → 316.** New: the halation scales are all exactly 1.0 and the shared-radius
fast path is taken on all 159; `radii_for()` really multiplies (probed off-database); Callier is
exactly inert at 0, inert on all 93 colour stocks at any setting, moves all 66 monochrome ones,
is the identity at dmin, and no stock carries Q < 1. Plus the two Coltman conversions are pinned
to the sine value and *away* from their printed CTF crossings, both directions, and the
8532-vs-8530 sharpness conflict is pinned so it cannot be averaged away.

**Guards rewritten rather than deleted.** Four assertions had become stale in the truthful
direction and each was inverted, not dropped:

* "8532 is not flagged `mtf_measured` — its panel is a CTF" existed to stop someone flagging a
  square-wave curve as an MTF. The job is now done properly, so the guard checks the other side:
  it must be flagged, and it must **not** have simply inherited the printed 37.78 crossing.
* the `mtf_measured` roster, 8 → 10, with the distinction between the eight Kodak stocks (three
  measured records each, from sine-wave panels) and the two Fuji ones (one measured record, from
  a square-wave panel) named as `_GREEN_ONLY_MEASURED` —
* — because the two C24 findings about **measured red** must exclude them. 8572's *transferred*
  red is 16.6 c/mm; leaving it in would have dragged the "measured reds cluster near 36 c/mm"
  claim to a 73 % spread and destroyed a finding that is about measured reds, in a different
  manufacturer's family than the one C24's anchor was derived from.
* the schema stamp, v10 → v11, in the two places that assert it.
