## 1. The extractor had to be repaired first, and that is the substance of this pass

Nine sheets were added. All four defects below returned **numbers that looked like MTF
measurements**, which is the only reason they are worth writing down.

**(1) One path, three curves.** The 1990s technical sheets emit all three records as a
single path object — the same hazard the granularity panels have and that
`granularity_vector.subpaths()` was written for. Read as one curve, H-1-5218 reported
`f50 = 69.7` off a trace that runs along blue, jumps to green and finishes on red. Fixed by
importing that splitter rather than copying it; a drawing that yields one wide subpath is
left exactly as it was, which is what keeps 5231, 5201 and 5274 reproducing their pins.

**(2) The log grid passes for a curve.** On 5245 and 5246 the grid is one connected
polyline, so the letter matcher handed it back as the **green record**: 7.8–477 cycles/mm,
response to 190 %, `f50 = 236.8`. Three shape tests now separate a transfer function from
a grid, and each is a true statement about the physics rather than a tuned threshold:

| test | what it asserts | curve | grid |
|---|---|---|---|
| single-valued | one response per frequency | median per-bin spread ≤ 12 % of frame | full height |
| falls | it crosses 50 %, so it covers the axis | 34–56 % of frame height | 0 |
| smooth | it descends | total variation / span **1.00–1.35** | **12.9** |

**(3) Label matching by nearest curve double-claims; by height at a common abscissa it gets
the order wrong.** On 5245 the letter "G" sits 4.4 pt from the **red** curve's end and 7.6 pt
from its own, took red, and the whole sheet fell back to the single-curve rule. Ranking by
height instead broke 5248, where red **stops** at 115 cycles/mm while green runs to 191 and
ends lower — that swap produced a red record **sharper than green**, which no colour
negative can be. Three curves and three labels is six permutations, so it is now solved as a
minimum-cost bijection instead of approximated.

**(4) A fragment has an f50 and it is meaningless.** 5293's red survives as a 30–125
cycles/mm arc starting at **53 %** response and reported `f50 = 32.0`. Intact curves in this
corpus all start at 96–110 %, so a record that starts low over less than a decade is now
**refused with a stated reason** instead of measured. ⚠ The first version of this guard
demanded 60 % coverage of the *frame*, and refused all eight sheets: Kodak rules these
frames to 600 or 1000 cycles/mm and draws the curve over the 2–100 the film can resolve. A
guard that rejects every real curve is worse than none.

**And the extractor gained the gate the other two plot readers already had.** `--overlay`
draws every traced point back onto the page. **All four defects were found by looking at
it**, and none by reading the numbers.

## 2. Hypothesis 1 — the per-record f50 ratio. Answered, and the RULE'S FORM is wrong

Seven stocks now carry a complete per-record measurement:

| stock | R | G | B | r/b | stored estimate | red was |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5201 VISION2 50D | 32.1 | 49.7 | 55.5 | 0.578 | *adopted 2026-08-20* | |
| 5274 VISION 200T | 35.4 | 68.8 | 74.0 | 0.478 | *adopted 2026-08-20c* | |
| 5217 VISION2 200T | 33.9 | 58.1 | 67.4 | 0.503 | 54 / 62 / 70 | **1.59× too sharp** |
| 5218 VISION2 500T | 37.6 | 54.6 | 69.7 | 0.539 | 42 / 50 / 58 | 1.12× (blue 0.83× too soft) |
| 5245 EXR 50D | 37.2 | 83.8 | 100.5 | 0.370 | 64 / 72 / 80 | **1.72×** (blue 0.80×) |
| 5248 EXR 100T | 37.4 | 75.1 | 111.2 | 0.336 | 62 / 70 / 78 | **1.66×** (blue 0.70×) |
| 5279 VISION 500T | 41.1 | 73.1 | 76.1 | 0.540 | 46 / 54 / 62 | 1.12× (blue 0.81×) |

Partial (red refused as a fragment): **5293** G 75.2 / B 114.6; **5205** G 55.9 / B 59.3.

```
red    32.1  33.9  35.4  37.2  37.4  37.6  41.1     mean 36.4, spread +-13 %
green  49.7  54.6  58.1  68.8  73.1  75.1  83.8     spread 52 %
blue   55.5  67.4  69.7  74.0  76.1 100.5 111.2     spread 70 %
```

⚠ **The finding is not "0.78 is the wrong ratio". It is that a RATIO IS THE WRONG FORM.**
Red f50 is **effectively a constant ~36 cycles/mm** across seven Kodak cine colour negatives
spanning 1989–2005 and three product families, while green spreads 52 % and blue 70 %. The
red record does not scale with the stock's sharpness at all — which reads, physically, as
the bottom record's sharpness being set by scatter through the two layers above it rather
than by its own emulsion. A rule of the form `f50_r = k · f50_b` cannot express that at any
value of k, and the measured k would be 0.34–0.58 anyway against the stored 0.78.

## 3. Hypothesis 2 — "q clusters by layer depth". Half confirmed, half REFUTED

C13 had recorded, off **two** stocks, that "both reds cluster at 1.84–1.89, both blues at
3.38–3.42" and that the rolloff exponent might therefore be **derivable** from the layer
stack. With seven:

| | n | range | mean | sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q red | 7 | 1.89 – 2.77 | 2.24 | 0.32 |
| q green | 8 | 2.26 – 3.23 | 2.69 | 0.35 |
| q blue | 8 | 2.38 – 3.42 | 3.08 | 0.37 |

* **The ORDERING is systematic: `q_R ≤ q_G ≤ q_B` holds on 8 of 8** stocks that yield two or
  more records. The deeper the record sits, the gentler its rolloff.
* ⚠ **The MAGNITUDE is not a per-layer constant, and the earlier tight clustering was a
  two-sample illusion.** Reds span 1.89–2.77, not 1.84–1.89. `q` also correlates only weakly
  with f50 (Pearson 0.39 over 23 curves), so it is not a proxy for sharpness either.

**So `q` cannot be derived for the 156 unmeasured stocks and must stay per-stock measured.**
That closes the question C2b was opened to ask — as a refusal with evidence rather than as
another open item. The `mtf_measured` flag keeping the unmeasured stocks on the legacy
Gaussian remains the right design.

The carrier itself holds up: the power law beats the Gaussian on **every one of the 26
curves**, by 1.1× to 5.8×, at rms 0.0095–0.132.

## 4. The non-Kodak sheet, and what it can and cannot settle

C24 asked specifically for other makers, because seven measurements of one maker cannot
establish a law. **The Agfa Vista sheet is the only non-Kodak MTF in the corpus**, and it
prints **one** curve, captioned *"Sharpness — MTF (Modulation Transfer Function)"* with
*"Densitometry: visual filter (Vλ)"* — a visual-weighted pooled response, not three records.

So it cannot test a per-record ratio at all. What it does establish is that the carrier and
the overshoot are not Kodak habits: **f50 50.0 cycles/mm, q = 2.63 at rms 0.039** (Gaussian
1.3× worse), overshoot **+11.7 %** at 3.4 cycles/mm — against a stored estimate of
56 / 63 / 69, i.e. the estimate is ~1.26× too sharp for the visual record too.

⚠ **Its four films are one sheet, not four stocks:** page 6's left column is Vista 200, which
the database holds; pages 5, 7 and 8 carry Vista 100/400/800, FUTURA II and CTprecisa, which
it does not. Only the Vista 200 panel is registered.

## 5. One measurement worth flagging on its own

**5279's sheet prints an adjacency overshoot of +42 % (green) and +55 % (blue)** at
15 cycles/mm — by far the largest in the corpus, where every other stock sits at +1 % to
+23 %. Verified on the overlay: the traced points sit on the printed curves, so this is the
document, not the extractor.

⚠ It also **makes that stock's rolloff exponent unfittable**: the carrier `1/(1+(f/f50)^q)`
equals 1.0 at zero frequency by construction, so it cannot represent a curve that starts at
1.42. Its fit comes back at rms 0.25 / 0.34 against 0.0095–0.132 everywhere else. Recorded,
not worked around — normalising the curve by its own peak for this one stock would put it on
a different convention from the other eleven sheets.
