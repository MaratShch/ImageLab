## 1. T4b — one legend, and the doubt resolves the other way

The figure had been traced and calibrated since 2026-08-27 and could not be used, because which of
its two curves was the CS41 process and which the CS2 was unknown — and adopting the wrong one would
have moved stored gamma by 12 %. Opened the PNG in the browser pane. The legend reads **"Cs41
Density"** on the red dashed trace and **"Cs2 Density"** on the black, with the annotation block:

```
Exposure: 3200K Tungsten 1/50 sec
Emulsion: 800T
Process: Cs41 vs Cs2
DlogH: (Ref = -1.535)
```

Re-fitted from the archived pixel rows with the assignment known: **Cs41 γ 0.5588** (rms 0.0127 D)
against **Cs2 γ 0.4911** (rms 0.0070 D) over log E −2.07…−0.08 — a ratio of **0.879**. Stored as a
third `ProcessVariant` with `gamma_scale=0.879`.

⚠ **A ratio, not a gamma, and that is not timidity.** The figure plots one density per process and
never names the channel, so no absolute gamma can be assigned to a layer — but both processes were
measured on the same unnamed channel under identical exposure, which is exactly the condition under
which a ratio transfers and an absolute does not.

⚠ **The recorded doubt resolves the other way.** The queue noted that a two-bath kit "ought" to be the
softer one, but that the red curve's 0.555 sat below CS41's own green-layer 0.621, so the story did
not close. With the legend read it does: Cs2 *is* the softer process, and the red curve reads below
the green layer because **it is not the green layer**.

⚠ **`dmin_shift` is 0.0 and that is a refusal, not a measurement.** The Cs2 trace reaches a clear toe
plateau at 0.575 D over 66 points; the Cs41 trace is still descending at its left end (0.604 D at
log E −3.20, 14 points, no plateau). The queue row's *"0.57–0.62 D for both"* read a plateau and a
falling curve as if they were the same kind of number.

## 2. C39 — the only open row with a live render defect, now closed

`ROLLEI_INFRARED_400` stores its sheet's **unfiltered** curve: peak 410 nm, 2.8 % of its energy past
700 nm. By that curve it is an ordinary panchromatic emulsion, so the gamut-reach guard passed it and
both engines derived a near-flat **(0.349, 0.315, 0.336)** for a film nobody shoots unfiltered,
against an authored red-dominant **(0.52, 0.20, 0.28)**.

**The fix is a carrier, not a threshold** — 0.028 is below every ordinary pan stock's own share, so
any threshold catching this one starts refusing Tri-X. New `TakingFilter` (schema v20), on two fields
because **two different facts needed separating**:

- `SpectralSensitivity.measured_through` — what the plotted curve already includes (provenance).
- `FilmProfile.taking_filter` — what the profile's look assumes in front of the lens (rendering).

On both IR stocks these differ: the curve is bare, the intended use is filtered. Conflating them was
the schema silence.

Applied in `film_sim` **before the guard and before the collapse**, in both `stored_layer_sensitivities`
(what the guards see) and `layer_sensitivities` (what the integration sees) — ⚠ filtering only the
first would have given a guard judging a filtered emulsion and weights derived from a bare one, and
on every stock the guard refuses that disagreement never shows.

Result: behind the sheet's own **715 nm** filter the peak moves 410 → **720 nm**, out-of-reach goes
0.028 → **1.000**, the guard refuses, and the authored triple is used. Mirrored in
`AlgoSpectralSensitivity.cpp` on both paths; `spectral_mono_parity` is **68/68 with zero guard gaps**.

⚠ **Only one of the two stocks can be fixed, and that is the honest outcome.** Both sheets name a
filter; only Rollei's prints a wavelength. Konica's TDSB-701 says "a Kenko R-1 red filter" with no
number — a *red* filter, cutting far shorter than 715 nm, so borrowing Rollei's figure would be wrong
as well as unsourced. Konica carries the designation with `model=""` and keeps being refused by the
guard on its own bare curve — but **by record now, not by luck**.

## 3. B4 — the blocker was two bugs in our own reading, neither in the source

The row said: *"THE BLOCKER IS AXIS CALIBRATION, AND IT IS NOT SOLVED… the gridlines do NOT divide
the labelled range into round steps — 14 vertical intervals across a 0–3 density axis and 29
horizontal across six decades."* Both counts were **one short**:

⚠ **The outermost gridline on each axis is fainter than the interior ones** — 457 and 819 dark pixels
against 461 and 819+ — and fell under the ink threshold. Lower it from 0.5 to 0.45 of the span and
p7 comes out **31 verticals over log E −4.00…+2.00** and **16 horizontals over D 0…3**: 0.2 per
interval on both axes, exactly round, frame corners at the labelled extremes. All four plates then
calibrate the same way, every one landing on a round step.

⚠ **And the embedded raster is stored upside down**, behind the page's own flip transform, which
`get_images` bypasses. That single fact produced *every* failure of the first working run, and not
one of them looked like an orientation problem: negative gammas, records stacked red > green > blue,
spectral peaks in reverse order, and a D-min trace that appeared to *rise* towards the red — which
the guard correctly reported as "the two traces are swapped". Three plausible diagnoses, one cause.

⚠ **A third bug the guards caught: every plate draws its legend in the same inks as its curves.** The
red record came out with a maximum of 2.83 D on a plate whose red curve never passes 1.5. Swatch and
curve are told apart by extent.

**Adopted:**

- **The orange mask.** Traced base+fog **0.173 / 0.531 / 0.962** against a stored 0.300 / 0.280 /
  0.290 — three near-equal numbers, i.e. *no mask at all*, on a masked colour negative. Every other
  masked negative in the file encodes it (CINESTILL_800T carries 0.187 / 0.526 / 0.876, within 0.09 D
  of these). 5247_1983 was the outlier.
- **The spectral dye density pair** into the v14 carrier, which had been empty — 31 samples each of
  Midscale Neutral and D-min on the standard 400–700 / 10 nm grid. That carrier was added in B1, by
  the same pass that noticed these plates and did not read them. Neutral+dmin pairs: **10 → 11**.
- **The spectral plate as a cross-check**: peaks 390 / 578 / 659 nm against the stored 390 / 560 /
  660, worst disagreement 18 nm. Agreement, so nothing changed.
- **The characteristic gammas as a cross-check**: 0.536 / 0.598 / 0.584 against 0.545 / 0.560 /
  0.580, inside 7 %. Refitting three hand-fitted ToneCurve shapes on that basis would be churn; the
  `[T1/T2]` tag's T2 half stands.

⚠ **REFUSED: the MTF, and `verify.py` caught the mistake before the reasoning did.** TI0835A gives
f50 = **46.9 cycles/mm** against a stored 24 / 28 / 33 flagged `mtf_measured=False`, and it looked
like exactly the upgrade the row promised. It is not: the plate is captioned **"Diffuse visual"** and
draws **one** curve, while `MTFSpec` holds three **per-layer** numbers. Writing one visual
measurement into all three is a category error — a colour negative's red record is the deepest and
most diffused layer, which is why it is the softest, and a visual MTF is dominated by the green.
Four separate checks fired and every one was right: *"every measured colour stock is softer in red
than the estimating rule"*, *"the measured red records of the NEGATIVE family stay clustered near
36"*, *"exactly the 16 vector-traced stocks are flagged mtf_measured"* — this plate is a raster — and
*"every mtf_measured stock carries a rolloff exponent"*, which a single visual curve cannot supply.
The number is recorded and not stored. What would let it in is a field for the film's overall visual
MTF, which the schema does not have.
