## 1. C1e — the item's own premise was wrong, and disproving it is what unlocked it

C1e existed because C1d suspected the raster granularity extractor of reading **1.32× high**
on the σ axis, and therefore refused to adopt anything off the four VISION3 technical sheets.

That suspicion is now **disproved, by the sheet's own instrument.** The 5219 granularity
panel prints a tick comb outside its right frame line; fitted on the stored calibration
(506.5 px at σ = 0.001, 140.25 px/decade) it reproduces **0.001 → 0.100 at all 15 ticks
within 1 %**, and 5203's comb within 1.3 %.

The 1.32× came from comparing the two documents at **absolute** D 1.0. Read at **net** 1.0 —
the convention C1b pinned, and the only one either document supports — the gap is:

| 5219, net 1.0 | R | G | B | b/g | r/g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| technical sheet, raster | 6.43 | 7.16 | 19.37 | **2.703** | **0.897** |
| brochure, vector | — | 6.37 | 15.45 | 2.425 | — |
| ratio between documents | | 1.12× | 1.25× | | |

A real document conflict, **a third the size claimed, and not an axis error.** Which means
the raster family's per-layer *ratios* are usable even though its absolute level stays
suspect — and ratios are what this adoption needs, because the green level was frozen.

### What was adopted

Ratios off each sheet, multiplied onto the stored pooled rms:

| stock | b/g measured | rms_r | rms_g | rms_b | was (heuristic) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5219 | 2.703 | **5.92** | 6.60 | **17.84** | 7.26 / 6.60 / 8.58 |
| 5207 | 2.123 | — | 4.20 | **8.92** | 4.62 / 4.20 / 5.46 |
| 5203 | 1.813 | — | 2.60 | **4.71** | 2.86 / 2.60 / 3.38 |

Three stocks, not one — 5207 and 5203 separate their blue track cleanly on the same sheets,
so refusing them would have been refusing a measurement that was already in hand.

⚠ **The brochure's 2.425 is recorded, not averaged** (method rule 4). The whole triple comes
from the **one** document that measures all three records; a brochure-blue / raster-red
hybrid would be a triple no document ever measured.

⚠ **Red is left unset on 5203 and 5207 on purpose.** 5203 draws its G and R granularity
curves as a single ink run at the left edge, and 5207's red track only begins at log E 2.5,
so their 1.05 and 0.85 are not measurements to adopt. Unset means the pooled figure is used
(r = g), which is not a measurement either — but it sits **between** the two sheets that do
separate red (0.85 and 0.90), while the discarded heuristic's 1.10 sits outside both, on the
wrong side of green.

⚠ **5213 stays on the heuristic, and a guard pins it there.** Its sheet prints the three
granularity curves as one overlapping bold band, so there is no blue track. Filling it from
its three siblings would be a class estimate from samples that span 1.5× between themselves
— and the sibling ratios track stock SPEED (500T 2.70×, 250D 2.12×, 50D 1.81×), which would
put a 200T near 2.0×: a plausible number with nothing measuring it.

## 2. C8 — the control is seconds, and the data was the bigger half

`AlgoControls` had **no exposure-time field at all**; `RenderSettings` had none either. Both
now carry one, defaulting to **0.0 = not stated**, which skips the stage entirely.

**Seconds, not shutter angle**, and the reason is measurable rather than stylistic: angle
divided by frame rate spans 1/1000 s…1/24 s, and every sheet in the corpus prints *no
correction needed* across exactly that span. An angle control would have been a knob that
provably never does anything. The corrections live where a still photographer works
(multi-second) or where a strobe does (tens of microseconds).

### Where it is applied, and why there

Inside stage 8, on the log exposure — after everything optical (taking filters, flare,
halation, emulsion MTF, record collapse) and before the characteristic curve. Reciprocity
failure is the **emulsion's** response to the light that reached it; folding it into stage 2
with `exposureStops` would let the flare and halation stages scatter light the lens never
delivered, and would make a long exposure move the halation threshold, which no datasheet
supports.

The shift lands on the **retained** log-E plane, so stage 8b sees the same effective exposure
the curve did. A real layer cannot tell the difference either.

### The model, and what it is not

```
table branch (21 stocks):  shift_c = -log10(2)·stops(t) + cc_credit_c(t)
spec branch (105 stocks):  shift_c = (p_c - 1)·log10(t / onset),  t > onset
```

Interpolated in log10 t — the axis the tables are printed on — and **held flat outside the
measured range** rather than extrapolated, because Kodak's own tables walk the effective
exponent from ~0.85 to ~0.70 across successive decades.

⚠ **My own C8 queue entry claimed the honest model is per-pixel intensity-dependent. It
cannot be.** All six pre-existing measured tables — and all fifteen new ones — are functions
of **time alone**; nothing on file carries an intensity axis. Real reciprocity failure *is*
intensity dependent (dark parts of a frame fail first, which is why a long exposure loses
shadow separation as well as speed), and that is exactly why the model is documented as a
per-channel **global** shift instead of shipping an invented exponent.

### 15 measured tables read from the sheets in this corpus

Because the exponent form was silently losing most of what the sheets say:

| stock | sheet prints | stored exponent delivered |
|---|---|---|
| 5205 / 5217 / 5218 | +2/3 stop @ 1 s **and** +1 stop @ 10 s, CC10R | 5205: +1/3 and +2/3 (**half**); 5217/5218: **nothing** (p = 1.0) |
| 5219 | +1 stop @ 10 s, CC10R (**brochure only**) | nothing |
| 5246 / 5274 / 5279 | +2/3 stop @ 10 s, CC10Y | nothing |
| 5248 | +1/3 stop @ 1 s, achromatic | nothing |
| 5201 | +1/3 stop @ 10 s, CC10R | correct already (0.90 @ onset 1 s) |
| 5231 | +1/2 stop @ 1 s | correct already |
| 5247 (TI0835) | +1/2 stop @ 1 s, CC10Y | correct already |
| 8532 / 8572 / 8547 | +1/3 stop @ 1 s, "nor filtration" | correct already |
| VISTA 200 | +1 stop @ 10 s, no CC row for negatives | 0.766 stop (**24 % light** — see below) |

**Seven stocks held a printed correction and rendered nothing.** A single Schwarzschild
exponent has nowhere to put an offset the film has already lost by 1 s: 5205's sheet prints
+2/3 stop at 1 s, and a slope-only fit anchored to the 1/3 stop per decade between its two
points reproduces +1/3.

⚠ **The CC10R / CC10Y split is printed, not inferred, and it flips across a product
generation.** The VISION2 family prescribes CC10R (red loses most); the VISION generation
before it prescribes CC10Y (red and green lose most, blue least). That is why the filter
letter is stored rather than a "colour negatives go warm at long exposure" rule.

### ⚠ An error of mine that the 5205 sheet caught

The CC-filter arithmetic first **added** the filter's density to the worst-losing record. So
"+2/3 stop and a CC10R" came out as **1 stop** on red. Channel ordering right, level wrong,
and entirely plausible in a frame.

The correct reading is that both instructions act on the same frame: the lens opens by the
printed stops on **all three** records, and the filter then takes part of that back from the
records it attenuates. So the record the filter does **not** attenuate loses exactly the
printed stops, and every attenuated record loses that **minus** the filter's density. A
`verify.py` guard now asserts it over all 21 tables, in the general form:

```
worst record's loss at the table's last time == that row's printed stops
```

It also falls out correctly for a compound prescription: a set attenuating all three records
(10Y + 10M + 10C) is a neutral-density filter, and crediting all three equally is what an ND
in the light path does.

## 3. Parity: a third family, on the plugin's own header

`cpp_parity.py` now compiles **the plugin's own `AlgoReciprocity.hpp`** — not generated code —
and compares it against `film_sim.reciprocity_log_shift()` over the whole database:

```
[i] reciprocity: 5724 probes over 159 stocks x 12 times, 1446 non-zero, 101 chromatic
[i] reciprocity: worst absolute disagreement 1.01e-07 decades
```

Times run 1e-5 s to 3600 s — **wider than every table on file, deliberately**, because the
held-flat behaviour outside the measured range is part of the law and both implementations
have to hold flat identically. `t = 0` is probed as the inertness case and must be exactly
zero on both sides. The family **skips** rather than fails when the plugin tree is absent, the
same policy `interimage_parity.py` uses.

Tolerance is **absolute** (1e-6 decades), not relative: the correct answer is exactly 0.0 for
most stocks at most times, and a relative tolerance against zero is meaningless.

## 4. Two defects recorded rather than silently patched

**(a) Two conventions in one table.** The Kodak/Fuji exponents were fitted as "the loss AT the
stated time" — which is what the sheets describe, since the compensation is made at the lens,
and it is what this law evaluates. The Agfa/Konica entries were fitted as
`t_a^p · onset^(1-p) = t_m`, i.e. "the film needs a longer exposure", giving
`p = ln10/ln20 = 0.77` for one stop per decade. Evaluated by this law that delivers **0.766
stops where the source prints 1.0 — 24 % light.** The corrected values follow directly from
the points those comments already record and are written out next to `_RECIPROCITY_TABLES`.

They are **not applied** because `PDF/PROFILES/KONICA/*` and `PDF/PROFILES/SOVIET STANDARDS/*`
are not present in the corpus this pass could read — only AGFA, FERRANIA, FUJI, GEVAERT,
KODAK, RETRO and SVEMA are — and re-deriving a stored value from a second-hand summary of a
document nobody can open is what method rule 14 forbids. **AGFA_VISTA_200 is the exception and
is fixed**: its printed point is on file in English, and it now carries a measured table that
overrides its exponent entirely.

**(b) Kentmere's tables do not reproduce their own stated formula.** PAN 100 stores 0.517 stops
at 10 s while its source string quotes `Ta = Tm^1.26`, i.e. `0.26·log2(10) = 0.864`; PAN 400
stores 0.599 against 0.997. Both ~40 % light. The HARMAN sheets are not in this corpus copy
either, so the traceable values stand rather than being bent toward a formula in a comment.
